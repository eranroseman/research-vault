# Requirements: sourcing

> Takes a requirement set, searches for existing components that might serve it, reports what each covers. Decides nothing. **Consumed by `writing-specs`**, with the requirement set.
>
> **Not confirmed.** Nobody was interviewed and nothing here has been agreed; no requirement carries an individual confirmation because none has one. Each traces to a ruling, a failure this project measured, or a primary — a provenance split by origin, narrower than R12's three classes upstream and sufficient here because nothing was inferred. Identifiers are never reused; gaps are retired numbers.

## Problem

What already exists gets asked at a different time and on a different clock than the requirements were written. Left inside the requirements phase, build-or-buy answers land in an artifact already agreed, and the search gets scoped to one need when it has others.

## Solution

Searches on what the requirements call essential, reports how it looked and what it found, describes each candidate against the whole set. Design screens, chooses and decides.

## Boundary

**Inside:** finding candidates, reading them, and reporting what each covers.
**Outside:** screening, ranking, choosing, rejecting, and building. Those are the design phase's, which sees the requirements and these findings together.

## Assumptions

- **What exists is findable.** S19 treats saturation as evidence the field is empty, which holds only if components are indexed, named in terms someone would search, and published somewhere reachable. A component that exists and is not discoverable is invisible here, and a run cannot tell that case from absence. S24's control query establishes the search tool works, not that the field is indexed. False the moment a candidate turns up that no search would have surfaced.

*The previous assumption — that a candidate whose body cannot be obtained cannot be described — is dropped: S8 already records such a candidate as not-examined, so removing it changed no run.*

## Requirements

### Reading the requirements

**S1** — Reads the requirement set, and asks about anything it needs and cannot find.
*Fit:* it needs which requirements are must-have and a version per requirement. Where either is missing or unclear it asks before searching. It never refuses and never guesses.
*Why:* a search aimed at a guessed must-have list looks like a search and is not one.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* author's ruling.

### Searching

**S19** — Searches on the must-have requirements only, from more than one source, until new sources return only candidates already seen.
*Fit:* where results are too many to report, it searches again adding the remaining requirements and says so. That narrows the search, never the candidates.
*Why:* searching on everything narrows the field before anything is read, discarding candidates for requirements they were never meant to satisfy.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* author's ruling; OTSO's saturation rule.

**S24** — Declares the search before running it, and reports what it did.
*Fit:* declared — the candidate concept, kinds of source, stopping rule. Reported — every source reached, terms used on each, where saturation fell, and any source or query added mid-search with its reason. Where a source returns nothing, how the run established the source and query were working.
*Why:* a null from an unrecorded method is not a finding about the field, and queries added after an unwelcome result are invisible unless declared.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* two failures here — a null trustworthy only because a control query happened to be run, and three queries added after three came back empty.

### Describing each candidate

**S16** — Records which requirements a candidate covers, which it does not, and which could not be determined.
*Fit:* undetermined is the default. Full assessment is claimed affirmatively; absent that claim, coverage reads as incomplete.
*Why:* this is the product.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* NTIA SBOM minimum elements.

**S4** — Describes the smallest part that could be taken without taking the rest.
*Fit:* a section, rule or step in a skill; a module or exported interface in a library; a clause in a standard. A whole candidate qualifies where the whole is that smallest part.
*Why:* a body dismissed whole takes its usable parts with it. This project paid that three times.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* author's ruling; PORE and MiHOS record at *(one requirement, one feature)*.

**S8** — Grounds every finding in the candidate's body.
*Fit:* a description may narrow the search; only the body grounds a finding. A candidate whose body was never read is not-examined, never covering or not covering.
*Why:* reading behaviour off a roster position was wrong about all three candidates it touched.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* that failure.

**S9** — Quotes text present in the candidate at the version named.
*Fit:* a mechanical check flags; only a read confirming absence voids. Where the read finds the text present, the run records what the check tripped on.
*Why:* a candidate was rejected here on two behaviours its artifact did not contain — and separately, three verbatim quotes failed checks on a soft hyphen, an HTML entity, and spacing.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* both failures; HALLMARK on verifiers over-flagging.

**S12** — Carries the reasoning behind each finding.
*Fit:* a reader can say why a finding went the way it did without asking. A finding whose grounds are absent from what it describes is void.
*Why:* design can overturn a finding only if it can see the reasoning.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* 24748-2 on 15288 §6.4.5.3(d)(3).

**S13** — Records the supplier and the originator where they differ, the licence, and the intellectual-property assertions.
*Fit:* due when a candidate is described, not when it is found. The licence is read from the candidate's own files and the run names which — a licence file, README, manifest or header. "None found" is sayable only after looking, with the places named.
*Why:* a candidate's terms bound what can be done with it regardless of fit, and a licence nobody looked for is not a licence nobody has.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* 12207 §6.1.1; SPDX takes the declared licence from the package's own files and bars a third-party label.

**S11** — Records what the body shows about fitting: inputs, outputs, invocation, harnesses.
*Fit:* what reading establishes. Exercising a candidate is design's work.
*Why:* coverage says a candidate could serve a requirement, not that it could be reached from here.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* 24748-2 — *"Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace… the analyses needed to confirm that interfaces are compatible."*

**S22** — Records capability the requirement set did not ask for.
*Fit:* its own finding, with its expected effect — helpful, neutral, or a cost.
*Why:* unasked-for capability is surface to understand and drift to track.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* MiHOS splits *OverMatch* from *Surplus* and prices both.

**S26** — Records where a candidate's own text is a claim rather than evidence.
*Fit:* a finding drawn from what a body says it does, rather than from what it demonstrably does, is marked as such. A description of behaviour is a claim; a worked example, a test, or an interface signature is evidence.
*Why:* a body can promise what it does not deliver, and S8 stops a finding resting on a README without stopping one resting on a body that overstates itself.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* abuse scenario. This project's own screen read three candidates' behaviour from roster position and was wrong; reading the body fixes the source and not the credulity.

### What it does not do

**S5** — Describes each candidate on its own, and decides nothing.
*Fit:* each candidate is described against the requirements alone, never against another: no ranking, no shortlist, no single answer. A per-candidate opinion is permitted and is evidence — which treatment a candidate's evidence points toward, for every candidate or none. Closing the decision is not.
*Why:* a skill that decides gives design a conclusion to accept rather than alternatives to weigh.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* author's rulings; 15288:2023 evaluates off-the-shelf items among design alternatives; FAR 10.002(d) leaves the choice downstream.

**S6** — Reports when nothing found covers a must-have.
*Fit:* stated with the coverage measured, editing nothing upstream and naming no decision.
*Why:* a null is an input to someone else's decision.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* author's ruling; FAR 10.002(c).

**S3** — Puts findings in its own artifact and leaves the requirement set untouched.
*Fit:* the set is byte-identical before and after, and the run names the path it wrote.
*Why:* the requirements were agreed before this ran.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* author's ruling; FAR 10.002(e).

### Handoff

**S14** — Hands on both the requirement set it read and its own findings.
*Fit:* design can reach every finding and its reasoning without re-running the search, and can overturn one. Handing findings over as settled fails.
*Why:* pre-searching equals searching inside design only if design still sees the alternatives and the reasons.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* author's ruling.

**S2** — Voids findings once a requirement's meaning has changed, and re-runs against the new meaning.
*Fit:* every finding records which version of which requirement it was scored against. Between runs, never within one.
*Why:* a requirement can narrow while keeping its number, and findings then look current. R22 and R4 both did this upstream.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* 29148 §5.2.8 Version Number.

**S25** — Runs on Claude Code and Codex.
*Fit:* the body invokes only mechanisms both provide, and a fresh install on each produces one complete artifact from one run.
*Why:* #56 recorded 8,873 bytes of guidance arriving absent on one harness with nothing raised.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* fails 29148 §3.1.7 as a constraint — supporting both could have been decided otherwise.

## Constraints

Externally imposed, per 29148 §3.1.7. Anything that could have been decided otherwise is a requirement.

- **A candidate's terms bound what can be done with it.** A component with no licence cannot be copied whatever it covers; only its idea can be taken, with credit.
- **MIT and Apache-2.0 attribution is owed wherever text is taken substantially.**
- **Archived on handoff, never maintained**, inherited from the workflow. A finding is true of a version, not of a project.

## Open questions

- **What shape is the findings artifact?** The requirements say what it contains, not how it is laid out. *Resolved by the first real run, which produces one.*
- **What is "actively maintained" worth?** The author's rule — *"I don't use verbatim a section from a skill with 1 user"* — has published thresholds for maintenance (Scorecard: one commit per week over 90 days) and none for adoption. No number was elicited. *Resolved when a candidate is found whose maintenance is the deciding factor; until then it costs nothing.*
- **Is the prior art deep enough?** Two standards were read before the requirements and five corpora after, which is the wrong order. Unsearched: COTS-selection literature beyond OTSO, PORE, MiHOS and CAP, and any second source for S9. *Resolved by a second prior-art pass, or by deciding the set is good enough to build against.*

## Evidence

Primaries read directly: ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017, 24748-2:2024, 29148:2018; OTSO (Kontio 1995), PORE (Ncube 2000), MiHOS, CAP; NTIA SBOM minimum elements; SPDX; OpenSSF Scorecard; FAR Parts 10 and 11; the AoA Handbook; HALLMARK.

**One null is load-bearing.** All five corpora were asked for a rule of the form *"screen in if it meets N of M requirements"*. None publishes one; they search on a designated subset and record everything else rather than gate on it.
