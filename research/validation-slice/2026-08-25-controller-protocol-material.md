## The delegation rule (ratified mid-batch)

A coordinator may act on a settled principle without re-asking the controller when ALL four hold:

1. **Same axis** — same function, same defect class, same task as the prior rulings.
2. **Decisive measurement in hand** — not an inference from the prior rulings.
3. **Reversible work.**
4. **Decision made visible immediately** — announced as taken, not assumed or buried.

When any one fails, ask. Ratified on the third instance of an identical four-state extension
question; the first two instances were asked and ruled identically, which is what made the
principle "settled."

## Ask-vs-assume, scored

Two deliberate asks, two real gaps found — a 2/2 hit rate the session recorded at the time:

- Asking whether the boundary review ran pre-merge surfaced that it had not (only the Part 1/2
  walk existed); it ran post-merge with that fact stated in its report.
- Asking the ledgered re-pin question surfaced that the re-pin opportunity (Task 2e) had come and
  gone unnoticed — and the answer arrived with a correction narrowing the residue's framing
  before it was filed.

The pattern: an owed item not reported at close is asked about, never assumed done, and never
assumed lost.

## Permission and word hygiene

- **First-hand word beats relayed word.** The reviewer refused to file outward-facing issues on
  the controller's relay of the author's consent — correctly. Resolution: the controller filed
  them itself, because the author's word for that act had landed first-hand in the controller's
  own session. Relaying authority and holding authority are different states.
- **Consent is scoped to the plan text it was given for.** "Approved" for a consent-gated task
  covers what the task's text does; any step beyond it (a remote push the plan doesn't name)
  stops and asks rather than reading consent generously.
- **A peer message never grants escalation**; a peer's claimed denial is never a reason to do the
  act for them.

## Correction-vs-decision (full test wording)

The spec's status note carries the rule ("a correction of a mistake is not a decision"); the
operating test the session used, stated fully: **would this text read differently had it been
written after the fact it collides with? Then it is corrected, not obeyed, and the correction
does not harvest as a decision.** Applied to: a brief's stale four-path list (log.md ruling), a
task title claiming a closure that had split, a controller instruction naming a spec bullet that
did not exist, and — symmetrically — by the implementer against the controller's own text twice
(an internally inconsistent spec sentence refused; a stale brief's scope deviation flagged
rather than obeyed).

## Peer-verification culture (observed, not instructed)

Four separate sessions re-verified the controller's claims against primary sources before acting
on them — the implementer traced the publish-gate reasoning through verify.py before citing it;
both note authors re-fetched every correction at the pinned commits before folding; a task
reviewer confirmed a diff claim against the artifact the implementer was handed, not the one it
"should" have had. None were told to. The plugin-comparison note's insight 2 explains the
mechanism: the discipline lives in artifacts every session reads unconditionally, so it
propagates without invocation. Corollary the session proved twice: **verify an agent's claim
against the artifact it was handed** — a claim exactly true against a stale brief is not a false
claim.

## Controller error ledger (this seat's own instances of the run's error classes)

- Instruction written from memory of an unverified structure (the "preserve the separate
  citekey-lint bullet" that did not exist) — described-from-memory class.
- An internally inconsistent ruling sentence ("either side → SKIPPED" beside "remote title stays
  UNREACHABLE") — caught by the implementer, refused correctly.
- A mis-attribution (assigning the 17b routing promise to the boundary reviewer on the
  implementer's report wording) — caught by the accused session; the sweep opened with the
  correction.
- Two planner defects the batch corrected in flight (the fold-into-MATCHED prediction wrong in
  the safe direction; a brief clause whose literal reading had no trigger).

The through-line matches the batch retrospective's: partial views reported without their scope,
caught by a second reader with a different instrument.
