# experiment-proposal compared with hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills

Date: 2026-10-23. Ours is `plugins/experiment-proposal` at 0.2.0, together with the v3 design spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-experiment-proposal-v3-design.md`, which is approved in brainstorming and not yet implemented. Theirs is [hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills](https://github.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills) at `1666e69` (tag v0.9.0, 2026-10-15). Our vendored pin is `a38b7b1`, an untagged commit (`v0.8.0-42`).

Paths for ours are relative to the research-vault root. Paths for theirs are relative to the upstream repository root and are marked "theirs". Files in the author's own proposal project are named as "the Bloom project's" file. Finding ids (scope-n, checks-n, review-n, lit-n, EVALS-n, agentdocs-n, governance-n) refer to the verified findings behind this report; where verification corrected a finding, the corrected wording is what is reported here. The saved lit-search rounds of 17 October and the 0.2.0 eval driver and scoring scripts are scratch files outside both repositories and are not durable.

## Summary

Theirs is an eleven-skill suite for an anonymous Bachelor's or Master's exposé that ideates, searches, writes, checks, reviews, supervises, customizes, imports, reverses, publishes and troubleshoots, measured by a routing rig and a multi-host eval harness; ours is a four-skill plugin for one course's controlled-experiment proposal that settles and freezes the design, checks the student's own draft item by item against a checklist, and hands off to an external defend stage. Their shipped rules (a five-section exposé, no supervisor names, ethics in two or three sentences, a required Statistical Analysis subsection) conflict with our course, so nothing of theirs should be vendored beyond the lit-search scripts, and their ideas should keep being restated into our checklist. The most valuable things to adopt are cheap: carry an earlier review round forward, re-vendor the scripts at v0.9.0, and add a placeholder report, a cited-versus-listed check and a record of the research questions a review judged. The v3 spec needs correcting before implementation: its scenario 8 would overwrite the four confirmed attestations and the waivers in the author's real `proposal/review.md`, its planned 429 backoff and output-size signal already exist upstream, its DBLP "defect" is our own query shape, and two frontmatter tests fail for certain under its section 1. We do better on settling and freezing design decisions, experiment and ethics criteria, a checker-validated review with a quote per pass, waivers carried into the defence, and run records tied to a commit; their MIT terms are met, though three of our files overstate how freely the six substance tests were restated.

## What to adopt

Ranked by value over cost. Tier 1 belongs in 0.3.0. Tier 2 fits 0.3.0 if time allows. Tier 3 is eval infrastructure, best done before the 0.3.0 re-runs. Tier 4 waits for a later release or a trigger. Items that change the v3 spec are cross-referenced from the next section rather than repeated there.

### Tier 1: in 0.3.0

#### 1. Carry an earlier review round forward

- What: when `proposal/review.md` exists, review reads it first. It keeps every `confirmed by the user` row and every waiver whose quoted passage is unchanged (an agent run never downgrades them to `not confirmed`), reuses the 3.1 values it records, marks each earlier fail resolved or open against what its fix asked for, and labels a new fail on unchanged text "missed in the earlier round". The 3.1 row records the confirmed values as the exact checker flags (`--sections "..." --words A-B --references A-B --supervisor "..."`), so the next run, interactive or not, uses them before the course defaults; the checker only needs its existing `confirmed by the user` match on that row.
- Why first: v3 scenario 8 runs agent-mode review on the Bloom project, whose `proposal/review.md` holds four confirmed rows (3.1, 5.2, 13.1, 13.4, dated 15 to 23 October) and 20 fix records. As written, the run would rewrite them as `not confirmed`, set Waivers to `- none`, and check the draft against the course defaults. Until this lands, run scenario 8 on a copy of the project.
- Where: `plugins/experiment-proposal/skills/review/SKILL.md` step 1 and its Agent run paragraph; `plugins/experiment-proposal/references/review.md`.
- Cost: medium. `review/SKILL.md` is at 998 of its 1,100-word cap, which only a spec edit can move, and it must also absorb the Agent run paragraph. The checker needs no change.
- Findings: review-2, scope-8, scope-9.

#### 2. Re-vendor the nine lit-search scripts at v0.9.0 before writing the wrapper

- What: copy `skills/proposal-lit-search/scripts/*.py` at tag v0.9.0 (`1666e69`). This brings arXiv over HTTPS with a feed-root parse guard, a nesting cap and retry; catch-all notes that name only the exception type; UTF-8 output on every platform; the `candidates:` size line; and snowball's per-seed default of 5. Four files change in content (`arxiv.py`, `common.py`, `search.py`, `snowball.py`); five need only a new sha in their header. Record the tag beside the sha, and from now on pin only to release tags.
- Where: the nine headers in `plugins/experiment-proposal/skills/lit-search/scripts/`, the hutzelmann row of `plugins/experiment-proposal/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`, the hutzelmann paragraph of `ATTRIBUTION.md`, and the `HUTZELMANN` constant in `tests/test_experiment_proposal_plugin.py`.
- Cost: small. The new scripts still pass the existing vendoring tests (no writes, the same four environment names, the same browser-verification heading). Our SKILL.md passes `--limit-per-seed` explicitly, so the new default changes nothing until item 6.
- Findings: governance-1, governance-2, governance-3, lit-2, scope-5, governance-13.

#### 3. A placeholder report in the draft checker

- What: `check_proposal.py draft --placeholders` lists each `[TODO...]`, `[TBD...]` and all-caps bracketed placeholder with its section and line; given a heading, it also compares that set with the list under the heading. It judges nothing and exits 0. Review reads it for item 13.5 and lists the open placeholders in the hand-off. Theirs warns only on its own `[TODO: ...]` syntax, so `[DATA STORAGE LOCATION]` would pass their checker too; the Bloom project's `proposal/notes/check.py` (its `[NAME]` and `[NAME: gloss]` pattern compared against Appendix H) is the better model.
- Where: `plugins/experiment-proposal/scripts/check_proposal.py`; review step 2.
- Cost: small; `check_draft` already parses headings.
- Findings: scope-7, checks-5.

#### 4. Cited-versus-listed check for numbered reference lists

- What: compare the `[n]` numbers cited outside References with the `[n]` entries. A citation with no entry is a defect feeding item 3.3; an entry nobody cites is an informational line (item 3.3 covers only the cited-to-listed direction). Reuse v3's bracket parser and its exclusions (code spans, scale anchors). Author-year drafts are out of scope. The Bloom draft passes, with 1 to 28 cited and 1 to 28 listed, only because its own `assemble.py` builds the list.
- Where: `check_draft`, plus two unit tests.
- Cost: small.
- Findings: checks-1.

#### 5. Record what a review judged

- What: `review.md` ends with the draft's research questions and hypotheses copied verbatim, and `check_proposal.py review` prints one informational line when they no longer match the draft; the hand-off then says a fresh review is due. Upstream added this after our pin (a3f654e). Prefer it to a whole-draft sha256: upstream rejected hashing on purpose (it fires on every polishing pass and cannot say what changed), and a digest would also go stale as soon as review step 6 applies the user's picked fixes.
- Where: `plugins/experiment-proposal/references/review.md` (one more allowed section) and `check_proposal.py review`.
- Cost: small.
- Findings: review-4, checks-7.

#### 6. lit-search rules in `SKILL.md`

Each is a sentence or two in `plugins/experiment-proposal/skills/lit-search/SKILL.md`; together they cost small.

- Snowball one seed per call at `--limit-per-seed 5`, replacing the 20-per-seed example. Every saved round of ours would have exceeded Claude Code's 30,000-character display (keyword rounds 34K to 54K characters, snowball rounds 278K and 362K), and step 3's "best two or three seeds" says nothing about one per call (lit-7, agentdocs-2, governance-3).
- Before round 1, seed the exclude file with the draft's existing references, and drop candidates already cited, by DOI or title. Upstream has this rule; the Bloom run had to invent it (lit-9).
- Judge each round's candidates together in one context, the only one that writes the exclude file: a preprint and its published version have different DOIs and are recognised only side by side (lit-13, agentdocs-3).
- Once every named need has a kept source, search further only for a need you name; a count below the target is reported in step 4, never a reason for another broad round. Keep the barren-snowball stop, which item 5.2 attests (lit-11).
- When every note reads as a network failure, say a sandbox is the likely cause; if the host has a fetch tool, query Crossref and arXiv read-only under the same rules and mark those sources as not fetched by the scripts (lit-10).
- Recommend `~/.config/thesis-proposal/api-keys.env`; if the user picks the project's `api-keys.env`, tell them to add it to `.gitignore` first. In the Bloom project `git check-ignore api-keys.env` exits 1, and our vendored `common.py` says a key file "must be gitignored by whichever skill creates it" (lit-12).
- Give DBLP a two- or three-term query of its own, and relay `dblp(0)` as "no record matched every term" (lit-1).

#### 7. A checker output contract: rule and item ids, and a channel for information

- What: `Defect` gains `rule` (for example `draft-words`) and `item` (the owning checklist id) in text and `--json`; review routes each line by its item field instead of by the informal message prefix. Informational lines (the reference-list total, the placeholder report, the review-currency note) go to stderr, as the frozen-brief note already does, so stdout and `--json` stay defects only.
- Where: `check_proposal.py`, review step 2, the checker tests.
- Cost: small.
- Findings: checks-3, checks-8.

#### 8. Anchors for the swap test, item 13.8

- What: in our own words, a named device, system or domain passes when it is what the study is about, constrains the method, or supplies data or access no comparable alternative offers; the check replaces or drops the name and compares claims, not nouns; method instruments and prior work are not judged here; the title is in scope. The Bloom review passed 13.8 by citing CARE and MITI, instruments upstream excludes, and never examined the domain in the title ("LLM Physical-Activity Coach").
- Where: `plugins/experiment-proposal/references/checklist.md` item 13.8. Keep the restatement credit in its Deviations section.
- Cost: small; the checker reads item ids only.
- Findings: review-6, governance-14.

## What the v3 spec should change

### Already done upstream: re-vendor instead of building or proposing it

These parts of the v3 plan already exist upstream, most of them since v0.9.0 and the 429 retry since before our pin. Re-vendoring (adopt item 2) is the better move than building them into `litsearch.py` or proposing them in a pull request.

- **429 retry.** `common.py` retries HTTP 429, 500, 502 and 503 twice, waiting 3 s and then 6 s, at our pin and at HEAD (`RETRIES = 2`, `BACKOFF_SECONDS = 3.0`). The "adds 429 backoff" half of the planned pull request duplicates it. What upstream lacks is a longer wait or one that honours `Retry-After` for Semantic Scholar's shared pool, and our own data does not show a longer wait would help (see section 3 below).
- **A truncation signal.** Since b5d24b5, `search.py` and `snowball.py` print `candidates: N (L lines, C characters on stdout)` on stderr, ending with a warning above 25,000 characters or 1,800 lines.
- **Snowball output size.** The default `--limit-per-seed` is 5, and upstream's SKILL.md snowballs one seed per call; one seed at 5 measured 17,657 to 23,735 characters, two seeds at 5 exceed the bound.
- **arXiv robustness and privacy.** HTTPS, a parse only when the first markup is the Atom feed root (so no document type or entity definition reaches the parser), a nesting cap of 32, a 16 MiB body cap, classified failures, and retry through the shared request loop (a721bfd). Catch-all notes now carry only the exception type, not server text.
- **UTF-8 output on every platform** (b311e4f).

Still ours and still needed at HEAD: `--exclude`, `--compact`, valid YAML out with every string quoted, the lenient parse (`_yaml_scalar` is unchanged at HEAD, so titles with embedded newlines still come out unquoted across lines), and the per-source summary with the query echoed.

Not an upstream defect: DBLP's empty results. DBLP ANDs every space-separated term as a prefix over title, author and venue and holds no abstracts; our four distinct queries had 6 to 8 terms. "health coach" returns 178 records and "health coach empathy" none. Drop the planned DBLP issue.
