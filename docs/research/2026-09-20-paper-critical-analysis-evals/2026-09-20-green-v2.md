### 2. Summary

- **Problem**: LLMs fabricate plausible references; reference managers store whatever they are given; database searches are manual; commercial detectors are paywalled. The objective is a free, open-source, web tool that verifies a reference against CrossRef, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex "within seconds," singly or in batch, and returns validated APA and BibTeX (Abstract; Section 1, p. 2). No research question or hypothesis is stated.
- **Method**: A **systems paper** by the calibration table in `references/method-menu.md`, with **no evaluation family**: nothing is manipulated, sampled, surveyed or analysed, so participants, pilot, sample size and design type are N/A. The evidence is the description itself. Section 3: a React/TypeScript web application in four modules (LaTeX filtering, BibTeX parsing, multi-source search, presentation). CrossRef is queried first (top 3 candidates), Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex as fallbacks (Algorithm 1, p. 4; Figure 1, p. 5). Title similarity is normalised Levenshtein distance on lower-cased alphanumeric strings (eq. 1). Author matching checks database family names for containment in the query; "fake authors" are capitalised query tokens matching neither title, journal, year nor any real family name. In fallback, author lists from the three sources are intersected: names confirmed in all three earn a +10 bonus, the rest are flagged "potential fabricated authors" (Algorithm 1, lines 14 to 28). Confidence is eq. 2 when title similarity exceeds 80 and author similarity is below 90, and eq. 3 (mean of four field similarities plus a bonus in [0, 10]) for structured input with high matching; each detected issue costs −10 to −20. Footnote 2 says thresholds were "empirically calibrated" on "known hallucinations." Section 4: quick and batch modes from one text box; sequential batch processing at 800 ms intervals; APA 7 and BibTeX regenerated from database metadata; download and clipboard export. Table 1 (p. 7) is a 13-row feature checklist against four reference managers: CheckIfExist is ticked on ten rows, the managers on none of the six validation rows.
- **Results**: None in the empirical sense: no test set, accuracy, precision, recall, error rate, latency or user data (N1 to N4, N19). The only numbers are third-party figures in Sections 1 and 2 (4,000 NeurIPS papers, 100+ hallucinated citations in at least 53 papers, 24.52% acceptance; citation error rates of 25% to 54%; hallucination rates of 6% to over 30%) and the tool's own parameters (rows = 3; thresholds 50, 70, 80; bonus 10; penalty ranges).
- **Discussion**: Section 5 lists five user groups (authors, LLM-assisted researchers, reviewers, publishers' submission pipelines, bibliometricians) and restates the gap and features. No limitations (N20), no future work, and, with no results, no account of why they came out as they did. The conclusion summarises the tool and reiterates its significance.
