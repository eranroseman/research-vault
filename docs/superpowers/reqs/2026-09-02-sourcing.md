# Requirements: sourcing (the build-or-buy phase)

> **For agentic workers:** this document consumes an **approved** requirement set and produces build-or-buy verdicts in its own artifact. It is one of two successors `writing-reqs` offers at its review gate; the other is `writing-specs`.
>
> **Status: derived, not elicited.** This set was not produced by the process `writing-reqs` specifies. Nobody was interviewed. Every requirement below is either a decision the author made explicitly while specifying `writing-reqs` on 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-02, or a method this project actually ran and can point at. **Anything that was neither is an open question, not a requirement.** As of 2026-09-02 every requirement but two also carries prior art, read from five corpora; **S2 and S9 carry none and say so.** The author's ruling is to build this skill first and use it on `writing-reqs` rather than the reverse, so this set is not waiting on an interview — it is waiting on its prior art being complete, and it now names where it is not.
>
> **Identifiers are `S`, not `R`,** and are never changed or reused. A red-team pass on the sibling document found INCOSE's own rule numbers sitting in a column of that set's identifiers, meaning two things at once; separate namespaces prevent it here.

## Problem

Deciding whether to build or adopt is a distinct act from deciding what is needed, and it was running inside `writing-reqs` as a third phase. Three things made that wrong. It has other callers — a build-or-buy question arises without a requirements interview at all, and #75's disposition survey was exactly that. It runs on a different clock — requirements settle once, sourcing re-runs whenever a requirement's meaning narrows. And its verdicts were landing inside an artifact that had already been approved, changing the thing after agreement.

## Solution

A skill that takes an approved requirement set, finds candidates that cover **enough** of it to be worth deciding about, and records what each one covers. It does not decide adopt, adapt or reject — that is `writing-specs`' call, made on the evidence this skill gathers. It never edits the requirement set.

## Assumptions

- **Candidates are readable.** Screening judges the artifact body — a skill file, a template, a licence — so a candidate whose body cannot be obtained cannot be screened. Nobody is obliged to publish one.

## Prior art

Two standards already held, read 2026-09-02, plus five corpora read for the competitive analysis below. **This is not a survey.** No COTS-selection literature was searched before the requirements were written; it arrived with the competitive analysis, which is the wrong order and is why several requirements were rewritten rather than written once.

What each source gave, and to which requirement, is on that requirement's *Source* line. Two findings are worth stating at set level because they shaped more than one requirement:

- **Make-or-buy is a trade-off, not a screen** (12207 6.1.1). It is supported by decision analysis and ends in an estimate, not a verdict — which is why this skill gathers evidence and hands the decision on.
- **Adopting does not excuse the analysis** (24748-2): *"Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace system feasibility analyses… needed to confirm that interfaces are compatible."* Install-as-is is the top rung of the ladder and the one most likely to be taken as a shortcut.

### Where the split sits, and the test for it

ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 **removed** the separate category buy-options used to have:

> *"The term 'Candidate Non-Developmental-Items (NDI)' including COTS (commercial-off-the-shelf), reuse of a previous design or acquirer provided items for obtaining system elements **has not been used and these have been included into 'system design alternatives'**."*

Read first as an objection to splitting sourcing out. It is not. What 15288 removes buy-options *from* is the requirements phase, and where it puts them is design — which is what this split did: sourcing no longer runs inside `writing-reqs`, and its verdicts no longer land in a requirement set.

The residual difference is smaller than it looked. 15288 has design generate and evaluate buy-options among its alternatives; here sourcing screens them first and design receives the result. Those converge **provided the design phase gets both artifacts**, which is why S14 exists. Sourcing pre-computes an analysis rather than removing it from design's reach, and S12's rationale requirement is what lets design overturn a verdict rather than inherit it.

**This is testable, but the test is blocked, not scheduled.** The test feeds `writing-reqs`' requirement set and its sourcing findings to `writing-specs` together. **`writing-specs` does not exist** — it is #75's vendored rename of `brainstorming`, and #62 owns its sequencing — so the test waits on that rather than on anything here. S14 is satisfied by producing a handoff, not by something consuming it, so nothing about building or running this skill is blocked. If `writing-specs` can design from the pair, the arrangement holds. If it needs to re-open a screened candidate, the pre-screening cost something and belongs inside design after all.

## Competitive analysis

Five corpora, 2026-09-02: COTS and OSS selection methods, open-source health metrics, procurement's participation gate, supply-chain dependency review, and design-alternative evaluation. Twenty agents, with every non-confirming finding sent to a verifier instructed to refute. **Twelve of fifteen were refuted**, mostly for generalising past their text — so the surviving findings are thin by design rather than by accident.

**The result that mattered was a null: no coverage threshold exists.** All five corpora were asked directly for a rule of the form *"screen in if it meets N of M requirements"*, and all five returned nothing, independently. Procurement's participation gate does not test coverage at all — it asks whether a party can perform. OpenSSF argues against aggregate scores outright. The COTS methods screen on a **named subset**, never a fraction.

That falsified a requirement rather than refining one. S15 had asked what the threshold should be; the answer is that the question was malformed, and asking it is what produced this project's failed screen. What the corpora supply instead converges from four directions and is now S15, S17, S18 and S16: elimination requires a **named critical flaw**, the screen runs on a **designated subset**, the survivor count is bounded by the **evidence budget**, and everything else is **recorded rather than disqualifying**.

**Limits.** Sixteen findings were initially held rather than tested, under a rule that turned out to be circular; they were mined on 2026-09-02 and five became S19–S23. One survivor remains declined — tiering requirements at approval time — because it contradicts the all-musts ruling and would force both this skill and `writing-specs` to transform the scheme before either could use it.

## Requirements

Each requirement names the ruling or the run it comes from. Nothing here is inferred from a standard that was not read.

### Input and ordering

**S1** — Checks the requirement set is screenable, and says so when it is not.
*Fit:* the run checks the set marks hard floors (S17 draws the screen from them) and versions its requirements (S2 voids findings scored against a superseded meaning). A set that is screenable is screened; a set that is not is **covered** under S16 and the run states which it did and why. Neither case refuses the work.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2
*Why:* Without it S17 and S2 reach for fields that may not exist and the run stalls with no way to say why. With it, a set from any source is either screened or covered, and the reader can tell which.
*Source:* the author's ruling of 2026-09-03, correcting this requirement rather than the run. An earlier S1 also required the set to have been **approved**, from the ruling *"reqs must be approved before sourcing begins."* That ordering is real, but it governs when the author invokes the skill, not what the skill polices — invoking sourcing and handing it a set **is** the approval, and no second act adds anything. It was also unrunnable: the fit read a verdict recorded by `writing-reqs` R32, which exists only on sets `writing-reqs` produced, while the ruling of 2026-09-03 requires this skill to work on sets from anywhere. Approval is provenance; screenability is form; the author's ruling was that this skill cares only about form.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02:** an approval gate before the study runs is prescribed in the design-alternatives corpus and repeated at criteria definition, and the same corpus expects criteria to move during a run without treating that as a violation — *"approved" means baselined, not frozen*. That supports the ordering, which stands, rather than a check the skill performs.

**S2** — Re-runs when a requirement's meaning changes, and voids the verdicts scored against the old meaning.
*Fit:* every finding records which version of which requirement it was scored against, reading the version `writing-reqs` R45 puts on it; a narrowing recorded under `writing-reqs` R19 marks each affected verdict void rather than stale.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 — "lets finalize reqs and then we redo all the sourcing" — plus R19's amendment, which was itself forced by R22 and R4 narrowing without retiring.
**Prior art, found 2026-09-02 in a standard already held.** The five CA corpora returned nothing, but nobody asked 29148. Its 5.2.8 attribute list carries **Version Number**: *"(and indication of the version of the requirement). This is to make sure that the **correct version of the requirement is being implemented** as well as to provide an indication of the volatility of the requirement."* And 29148 §5.2.9 gives the warrant for acting on it — traceability is *"fundamental to performing coverage analysis… compliance analysis… and **impact analysis when requirements change**."* 12207 6.3.5 supplies the surrounding machinery: baselines *"form the basis for subsequent changes"* and configurations change through controlled release of a new version. The version belongs on the requirement — `writing-reqs` R45 — and this requirement is what consumes it.

### Screening

**S17** — Names which requirements form the screen, before screening, and screens on those alone.
*Fit:* the artifact lists the screening requirements and why each was chosen. It does not set a bar per requirement: R43 delivers a binary and S15 forbids eliminating on anything graded, so there is no scale for a bar to sit on. The only choice made here is which floors enter the screen, written before any candidate is judged, and each is drawn from the hard floors the requirement set marks, whatever produced that set. The screen may be narrower than the hard floors; it is never wider. **Where a set marks no floors, the run says so and screens on nothing** — every requirement is recorded as coverage under S16 and no candidate is eliminated. Requirements outside that list are recorded per candidate under S16 and never eliminate.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the same pass. Every COTS method surveyed screens on a designated subset: the General COTS Selection method — *"Filter search results based on a set of 'must-have' requirements in order to define a short list of COTS candidates to be evaluated in more detail"*; PORE — *"reject products which are non-compliant with **essential** customer requirements"*; OTSO — *"It is enough to define the search criteria broadly so that the search is not unnecessarily limited by too many constraints"*, with screening then run *"based on the same criteria that was used in the search process"*. Ncube states the corollary: *"requirement specifications need to be sufficient to enable effective product selection rather than complete with respect to the user's needs."* **Corrected 2026-09-02.** An earlier S17 had the screener invent the tiering. 29148 5.2.8 makes priority a requirement attribute whose stated purpose is naming *"what requirements are candidates for the trade space when decisions regarding alternatives are necessary"* — which is this step. So the set marks what cannot be traded and the screening subset is chosen here within it. **This skill cares about the form of a requirement set, never about what produced it** — `writing-reqs` R43 is one way floors get marked, not the only one, and a set from any source that marks them can be screened against. S15's critical flaw reads the same marker: a candidate failing a hard floor has one, wherever the floors came from. The author's ruling that *"must and optional is relevant for sourcing"* holds at the grain it was made — the tiering is **this skill's**, made here and recorded here, not inherited from a requirement set that is all musts by construction.

**S15** — Eliminates a candidate only for a named critical flaw, never for incomplete coverage.
*Fit:* every elimination names the flaw and the evidence for it, and the disqualifier is **binary and verified** — a graded or scored signal informs a finding and never removes a candidate. "Covers too few requirements" is not a flaw and does not eliminate. A run that eliminates every candidate without naming a flaw for each has failed rather than found nothing.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1
*Source:* five corpora read 2026-09-02, converging. The rule is the DoD Analysis of Alternatives Handbook §9.1.1: *"The first screening eliminates non-viable alternatives, i.e. alternatives that have a critical flaw. **An alternative should not be considered 'non-viable' because it fails to close 100 percent of the shortfall.**"* Singer, Doerry and Buckley put the burden on elimination rather than retention — *"Eliminate those proven inferior to others. Use low cost tests to prove infeasibility or identify Pareto dominance… This ensures that we reduce the set of options based on additional information and not on arbitrary decisions."* MiHOS records total non-satisfaction without eliminating: *"a Decline type does not refer to rejecting a COTS, but indicates a technical goal that is fully not satisfied by a COTS product."* An earlier S15 asked for a coverage threshold; **no such threshold exists in any corpus read**, and PORE's own literature review names the failure it causes — current criteria *"may lead to unjustified elimination of candidate products that might have provided reasonable subset of the proposed system functionality."*

**S18** — Bounds the number of candidates carried forward by what can actually be evaluated, and says so in advance.
*Fit:* the run states how many candidates it will examine in detail and why that number, before screening. A candidate dropped to meet that bound is recorded as not-examined, never as failed.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the same pass. OTSO gives the reason — *"Evaluating and analyzing all the relevant characteristics of any one alternative takes a non-trivial amount of time, typically more than the organization has available… Therefore, it is both necessary and cost-effective to select the most promising candidates for detailed evaluation."* PORE prints the counts into its process (n → 3–6 → 2–3); MiHOS went 32 → 5 on nineteen screening rules; CAP plans survivor counts before screening as a measurement-cost calculation. The FAA states the same warrant: *"When numerous possible alternatives are identified, a detailed analysis of each one may not be cost effective."* **The bound is the budget, never the fit.**

**S19** — Searches from more than one source and stops when new sources return only candidates it has already seen.
*Fit:* the finding names every source searched and where saturation was reached. A run that searched one source, or that stopped without saying why, fails.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* OTSO, read 2026-09-02 — *"One main challenge in the search is the difficulty of deciding when to stop the search: how do you know that you have searched enough and found all the relevant alternatives? A simple strategy for ending the search is to use several sources."* Nothing else in this set bounds the **search**; S18 bounds how many candidates are examined in depth, which is a different question. Held back from the competitive analysis by a test-not-generator rule and applied 2026-09-02.

**S20** — States the search budget it worked to and where it stopped.
*Fit:* a reader can tell a short candidate list from a verdict on the field. A finding that reports few candidates without saying how far the search went fails.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* FAA NAS SEM §4.6.3.4 — *"When numerous possible alternatives are identified, a detailed analysis of each one may not be cost effective; therefore, down-selection of candidates is recommended."* The budget, not candidate merit, is the only warrant for narrowing, so an unreported budget makes the two indistinguishable. **This is the requirement whose absence produced this project's worst sourcing claim:** *"nothing exceeds three of twenty-nine"* was read as a fact about the field when it was an artifact of the method.

**S7** — Screens each candidate in isolation, without knowledge of the others.
*Fit:* no screening run can name a candidate it was not given, rank candidates, or recommend one.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the method this project ran on 2026-09-01, recorded in the sourcing screen. Twelve candidates, one isolated agent each.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02:** both UK instruments make a condition of participation a property of the supplier measured against the contract, *with no reference to any other supplier*. The underlying distinction is what makes this correct here — **capability is absolute, preference is relative** — so isolation is right at screening and wrong at selection.

### What it records

**S3** — Findings live in this skill's own artifact, never in the requirement set.
*Fit:* the requirement set is byte-identical before and after a run, and the run names the path of the artifact it wrote. The first half is the prohibition; the second is the obligation, and a run satisfying only the first has produced nothing.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 (Q19). Keeping them in the requirement set would change an artifact after it was approved, which is what approval-before-sourcing exists to prevent.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02, partial and honest about it:** FAR 10.002(e) mandates that search results be documented as their own output, and FAR's ordering makes co-location with the requirements document impossible in the normal case, since research runs *"Before developing new requirements documents"*. The separateness is a **consequence of the ordering, not a stated prohibition**. The design-alternatives corpus keeps findings in a dedicated report artifact distinct from the requirement baseline it consumed, and the health corpus separates what an artifact's authors assert from what an assessor concluded — the same separation one level up.

**S4** — A finding names the smallest part of a candidate that could be taken without taking the rest.
*Fit:* the unit follows the candidate's kind — a section, rule, gate, table or step in a skill; a module, function or exported interface in a library; a clause in a standard; a field in a template. Reading the body under S8 is what identifies them. A finding may name a whole candidate where the whole *is* that smallest part, or where the whole is what would be taken. **No finding rejects a whole candidate**: a part failing a requirement says nothing about the rest of the body.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2
*Why:* Rejecting a body for one failed part throws away the parts that work, and there is no way to get them back later except by hand. This project did exactly that three times.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-01 — "the fact that a skill violates one requirement doesn't mean it has another part that fulfils another requirement better than any other skill." Applying it moved `requirements-clarity` from Reject to Adapt: its 90/100 score gate was declined and its decline-signal list taken. `write-spec` and `interview-me` were recovered the same way.
**Version 2, 2026-09-03**, on the author's questions — why, how, and what happens when the requirements are for a library. Version 1 said "names a component, never a whole skill" and never defined a component, which left the unit unstated and the rule unusable outside skills. Two changes: the unit is now defined by separability and instanced per candidate kind, and the prohibition is narrowed to **rejection**, which is what the 2026-09-01 ruling was about. Version 1 also forbade a finding about a whole even where the whole is what would be adopted, so "install this library as-is" was unsayable.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02, and it goes finer than this requirement does:** PORE's compliance sub-model and MiHOS both record at the pair *(one requirement, one feature of one product)*; neither can express a finding about a product as an undifferentiated whole. Both SBOM standards are built on the component as the unit, and CycloneDX anchors evidence to locations *within* a component. The design-alternatives corpus prescribes, for a rejected whole, building a new candidate out of its usable parts — **so the hand-recovery of three candidates was not a workaround, it is the documented method**.

**S16** — Records, per candidate, which requirements it covers, which it does not, and which could not be determined.
*Fit:* a reader can say for any candidate and any requirement which of the three it is, without re-reading the candidate. The run states affirmatively where a candidate's coverage was fully assessed; absent that statement, coverage reads as incomplete rather than as absent.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1
*Source:* follows from S15 — an inclusion decision needs a coverage map, where a disqualification needed only the first failure. The third state is NTIA's SBOM minimum-elements rule, read 2026-09-02, which makes *unknown* the default reading and puts the burden on the recorder to claim completeness rather than letting silence imply it. The earlier screen recorded claimed passes and a failure count, which cannot answer the question `writing-specs` will ask.

**S21** — Records a candidate that narrowly missed a disqualifier, or that would qualify under a requirement stated differently, and returns it rather than dropping it.
*Fit:* each such candidate is recorded with its margin, or with the restatement it would need, and is handed to `writing-specs` and to the requirement set's owner. The skill does not restate the requirement itself.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* FAA NAS SEM §4.6.3.5 — *"Identify any alternatives with high-weighted scores that narrowly failed the pass/fail criteria. Discuss these alternatives with the stakeholder."* FAR 10.001(a)(3)(ii)(C) makes "would meet the need if the requirement were relaxed" a screening-**in** category, and 10.002(c) supplies its return path. This closes the open question about whether a near-miss feeds back: it does, as information, and the decision stays with the owner.

**S22** — Records, per candidate, capability the requirement set did not ask for, and whether it is expected to help, be neutral, or cost.
*Fit:* excess is recorded as its own finding, not folded into coverage. A candidate carrying twice the surface area needed is not thereby better.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* MiHOS, read 2026-09-02, which splits it — *"OverMatch: A COTS feature exhibits more capability than what is required… Surplus: There is an extra COTS feature which is not required"* — and treats the excess as having a positive, neutral or negative effect. S16 records what a candidate misses and nothing recorded what it carries unasked. For one developer the cost is concrete: surface area to understand, and drift to track.

**S12** — A finding carries its rationale, not only its result.
*Fit:* a reader can say why each verdict went the way it did without asking, and a verdict whose stated grounds are absent from the artifact it judged is void.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024 on 15288:2023 6.4.5.3(d)(3) — rationale kept *"retrievable and accountable"*. Read 2026-09-02. Forced by measurement, not preference: the 2026-09-01 screen rejected spec-kit `/specify` on two behaviours that re-reading the same day found in neither current preset.

### Gathering evidence about a candidate

**S8** — Judges the artifact body, never a description, README or roster position.
*Fit:* two steps, and only the second grounds anything. A description, README or registry entry **may narrow the search** — that is what descriptions are for. A **finding** quotes the body it judged and names every source it consulted; one resting on a description fails, which is the failure this requirement was written from. A candidate whose body was never read is recorded as not-examined under S18, never as covering or not covering.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the same run, and the failure it was correcting: an earlier pass read a candidate's exit path off its roster rather than its body, and was wrong about all three candidates it checked. The two-step shape is the author's, 2026-09-03, and it is how skills themselves work — a description routes, a body does. Discovery stays cheap; judging stays honest.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02, by two routes.** The roster clause has a direct anchor: Procurement Act 2023 s.22(4)(a) forbids building a condition of participation on a candidate's presence in a prior-award list — being on someone's list is not evidence of capability. The body-over-description clause is supported by admission rather than by prescription: OpenSSF Scorecard disclaims its own checks as heuristics with false positives and negatives, and the OpenSSF's own comparison concedes it measures what is automatable rather than what matters, with the badge depending on self-reporting.

**S9** — Every claim about a candidate quotes text present in that candidate at the version named, and a finding whose quotation does not check is void.
*Void, defined once and used by S2, S9 and S12:* the finding does not count toward anything, and the run names what must be redone to replace it.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Fit:* re-reading the named artifact at the named version finds the quoted text. Where it does not, the finding is void and the candidate returns to be screened again — **it is not eliminated**, because a failure of the screener is not a flaw in the candidate (S15). Each check records what was tested and what it returned, including *not applicable*, rather than a bare verified.
*Source:* a measured failure in this project, and only partly supported by prior art. The failure: the 2026-09-01 screen rejected spec-kit `/specify` on two behaviours — a hard error on empty input and a three-marker clarification cap — and re-reading both presets the same day found neither. The artifact existed; the cited behaviour did not.
**What the prior art covers, and what it does not.** `sources/` holds four 2026 papers on LLM citation hallucination, read 2026-09-02. All four address **existence** — whether a cited work is real. HALLMARK decomposes validity into *"six sub-tests (values: True/False/N/A)… independently verifiable dimensions: (1) DOI resolves, (2) Title exists in bibliographic databases, (3) Authors match…"*, and Zhao et al. measure the problem at scale in published arXiv papers. **None addresses the failure this requirement is for**, which is a source that exists and does not say what was claimed — support rather than existence. Citing them as support for the rule would be the stretch this project has made three times already.
What does transfer is the **shape**: decompose the check into named dimensions, record what each returned, and carry an explicit not-applicable rather than letting silence mean anything. That is HALLMARK's method and it is credited as a method, not as evidence for the rule. *An earlier S9 required recording a per-candidate date of reading; that was withdrawn on 2026-09-02 after the supply-chain corpus showed NTIA prescribes exactly one document-level timestamp, making the recorded single date correct practice rather than the failure this requirement was built on.*

**S10** — Reads licences from the licence file, not from a platform's detected label.
*Fit:* the licence recorded under S13 names the file it was read from, or records that none exists. This binds the licence finding, not every finding — a finding about behaviour has no licence file to name.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the same run — `framing-doc` was found to have no LICENSE file at all, verified rather than inferred.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02, directly:** SPDX exists in this shape because a platform label is not trusted as the licence of record. It splits the field — the **declared** licence must come from text in the package's own files, and information from a third-party repository is barred from it by name; a detected label may enter only as a **concluded** licence, which is the document creator's own analysis and must be explained.

**S13** — Records the supplier and the originator where they differ, the licence, and the intellectual-property and copyright assertions.
*Fit:* due when a candidate enters the screen, not when it is found — discovery is cheap and this is not. Each is then named per candidate, or recorded as absent; "MIT" alone does not satisfy it. Who distributed a component is routinely not who made it, and both SBOM standards split them for that reason.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017 6.1.1 — for COTS and open-source software, acquisition *"can be limited to identifying the supplier, accepting or negotiating the conditions in a pre-defined licence… determining rights to intellectual property and data rights."* Read 2026-09-02. S10 covered only the licence.

**S11** — Names the compatibility check it ran at the seam, not only the requirements a candidate covers.
*Fit:* every included candidate states what was checked at the seam — invocation, inputs, outputs, and the harnesses under `writing-reqs` R34 — and a verdict claiming a pass with no seam check fails.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024 — *"Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace system feasibility analyses or the risk reduction analyses and effectiveness assessments needed to confirm that interfaces are compatible."* Read 2026-09-02. This project's own screen measured requirement coverage and never checked a seam.

**S5** — Gathers the evidence each treatment on the ladder would need, without choosing one.
*Fit:* for every candidate, the artifact carries what a downstream decision would require to pick among install as-is, fork and keep merging, copy frozen, copy plus a delta, author to the design and credit it, or write from scratch. **A per-candidate opinion is permitted and is evidence**: the run may say which treatment a candidate's evidence points toward, and may say so for every candidate or for none. What it may not do is **close the decision** — name one candidate as the answer, or state an outcome the design phase is expected to accept. Five candidates each carrying an adopt recommendation is a richer input than five bare coverage tables; one candidate named as the choice is a decision this skill does not make.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the author's rulings, 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-01, supply the ladder — "the best option is to find a plugin that has all the skills we need that we can install and use as is", "less changes is better, homebrew is last resource", "there should be 'copy plus delta', it isn't finalized". The author's ruling of 2026-09-03 draws the line inside that: an opinion per candidate is another input for the decision, not the decision — which is the shape S23 already uses when a health signal *argues against* a treatment. The author's ruling of 2026-09-02 moves the **choice** downstream: sourcing's job is "allocating sources that qualify for enough reqs so writing-specs can make adapt/adopt/reject decision for them and their components." An earlier version of S5 had sourcing issue the treatment itself.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02, split down the middle.** The evidence-gathering half is strongly supported: OTSO's cost-to-baseline estimates what moving a candidate up the ladder would cost, MiHOS forecasts anticipated fitness under a resource cap, and FAR 10.002(b)(1)(ii) makes the cost of modification a required output. The *without choosing one* half is supported by two corpora and contradicted by one — procurement puts the choice with the contracting officer at 10.002(d), downstream of the research, and the design-alternatives corpus permits an analysis to complete without a recommendation; but **every COTS method surveyed ends in a selection and none separates the finder from the decider**. **The six-rung ladder itself has no support in any corpus read** — nothing maps evidence types onto install-as-is / fork / copy-frozen. S5 stands on the split, not on the ladder.

**S23** — Records each health signal against the ladder treatments it bears on, recording the direction each signal argues in, without marking any treatment unviable.
*Fit:* no health signal eliminates, and none rules a rung out. A signal is recorded as arguing for or against a named treatment — "argues against install-as-is and keep merging" — which S5 forbids converting into a choice. A maintenance signal is recorded with the window it was measured over — for example at least one commit per week over the previous 90 days, archived, or younger than 90 days and therefore unassessable.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* **hypothesis, marked as such.** OpenSSF Scorecard supplies the thresholds — *"If there is at least one commit per week during the previous 90 days, the project receives the highest score"* — and the counterweight the author's rule needs: *"a lack of active maintenance is not necessarily always a problem. Some software, especially smaller utility functions, does not normally need to be maintained."* The routing itself is not published: that low maintenance argues for *copy frozen* over *install as-is and keep merging* is inference from the ladder's own shape. **It is the only thing in this set connecting evidence to a rung**, which the prior art otherwise leaves unsupported, so it is worth having and worth doubting.

**S24** — Records what it searched for and in what terms, so any result can be re-run.
*Fit:* the artifact states the concept a candidate had to match, and for each source the exact terms or queries used. A null result from a source whose terms are unrecorded is not a finding about the field and may not be reported as one. Where a source returns nothing, the run says how it established that the source and the query were working.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* the by-hand run of 2026-09-02, which produced a null from a GitHub search on three unrecorded queries. The null was only trustworthy because the run happened to test the search tool on a control query first — a step nothing required and nothing recorded. This is S9's principle applied to the search rather than to a candidate: a claim nobody can re-run cannot be checked, and a null is the claim most likely to be wrong and least likely to be questioned.

### Handoff

**S6** — Reports that no candidate qualified, and does not decide what follows.
*Fit:* a run finding nothing emits that as a finding with the coverage it did measure, edits nothing upstream, and names no build decision.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1
*Source:* two author rulings, 2026-09-02. "If you can't source anything, then you develop" settles that the requirements do not change — an earlier version imported FAR 10.002(c)'s re-evaluation clause, which exists because government procurement has a statutory preference for commercial items, and no such preference operates here. The decision to develop is `writing-specs`', not this skill's, on the same ruling that moved the treatment choice downstream.
**Prior art, 2026-09-02:** FAR 10.002(c) and (d)(2) both take *"nothing suitable was found"* as an input to someone else's decision rather than as a verdict the searcher draws, and reg 65(7) does the same for a shortfall. The design-alternatives corpus names three responses to a null — relax a criterion, look for more candidates, report it — and **all three are owned by the stakeholder, not the analyst**.

**S14** — Hands both artifacts to `writing-specs`: the approved requirement set it consumed, and its own verdicts.
*Fit:* the design phase can reach every verdict and its rationale without re-running the screen, and can overturn one — a run that hands over verdicts as settled rather than as evidence fails.

It is what makes this arrangement converge with ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023, which evaluates buy-options among design alternatives rather than ahead of them; pre-screening is only equivalent if design still sees the alternatives and the reasons.
