## 2. Summary

- **Problem**: the paper states no research question and no hypothesis; its objectives are practical. It sets out to provide immediate verification of bibliographic references against multiple scholarly databases (abstract; §1 para 1, p. 1), to close the gap it argues exists between reference managers that organise without validating and commercial detection services behind freemium limits (§1 para 4, p. 2), to support both single-reference and batch BibTeX verification through one interface (§4 para 1, p. 6), and to return corrected APA and BibTeX records derived from database metadata rather than from the user's input (§4 para 3, p. 7).

- **Method**: no family from the method menu fits, because the paper runs no study — there is no experiment, no correlational observation, no survey, no archival analysis and no qualitative design. The Paper Type recorded at stage 1 therefore stands in its place: **systems**. The paper has no evaluation, so there is no evaluation family to name either. What that implies about the results is the central fact of this report: the paper can show that an artefact exists and how it is put together, and it cannot show that the artefact detects anything, at any rate, or better than any alternative. The Summary slots that `references/quantitative-methods.md` specifies for an experiment — participants, materials, procedure, design, independent and dependent variables, pilot, sample size relative to similar published work — have no referent here, and that file is loaded for a different reason: the paper reports quantities it computed and calibrated, so its reporting standards bear on the equations and thresholds rather than on a design.

  The process followed, as the paper describes it: a React and TypeScript web application separated into four modules — LaTeX-command input preprocessing, BibTeX parsing, a multi-source search service, and a presentation and export layer (§3 para 1, p. 3). Verification cascades through three databases: CrossRef as primary, queried for three candidates; Semantic Scholar as substitute when CrossRef returns nothing and again as fallback; OpenAlex completing the cascade (§3 para 2, p. 3; Algorithm 1 lines 2, 4, 10, 11, p. 4). Matching combines normalised Levenshtein title similarity after lowercasing and stripping non-alphanumeric characters (equation 1, p. 6), author matching by family-name extraction and containment checking in the query string (§3 para 7, p. 6), and journal and year matching (Figure 1, p. 5). Scoring is a composite confidence: equation (2) for the case of title similarity above 80% with author similarity below 90%, equation (3) averaging title, author, journal and year similarity plus a multi-source bonus, with penalties of -20 for a title mismatch, -20 for an author mismatch, -10 to -20 for a journal discrepancy and -10 to -20 per detected fake author (§3 para 8, p. 6). Control thresholds as printed: fallback at bestScore below 70 or any detected issue, a +10 bonus at two or more cross-source confirmed authors, an existence verdict at bestScore above 50 (Algorithm 1 lines 8, 22, 32), and gates at 70% and 80% in Figure 1. Footnote 2 (p. 6) states that the thresholds and penalties were empirically calibrated. Batch mode processes entries sequentially at 800 ms intervals (§4 para 2, p. 7) and outputs APA 7th-edition citations and BibTeX records with generated keys, exportable by download or clipboard (§4 para 3, p. 7).

- **Results**: the paper reports none. There is no analysis, because there is no data: no descriptive statistics, no tables of measurements, no charts of results, and no inferential statistics. Table 1 (p. 7) is a thirteen-row feature-presence matrix over five tools, not a measurement. Equations (1) to (3) are definitions, not findings. The only quantities the paper reports about its own system are its thresholds (50, 70, 80), its penalties (-10 to -20), its bonus range ([0, 10]) and its 800 ms batch interval; every other number in the paper — 140 million CrossRef works, 200 million Semantic Scholar papers, 25% to 54% citation error rates, 6% to over 30% hallucination rates, 4,000 papers, 100 citations, 53 papers — is quoted from a cited source, and two further figures, the 24.52% acceptance rate and the 21,500 submissions, are quoted from no source at all. The inferential-statistics slot that `references/quantitative-methods.md` describes is empty for the same reason: nothing was tested.

- **Discussion (the authors' own)**: the authors interpret the system as complementary rather than competitive — it "does not seek to replace comprehensive tools like Zotero but rather provides a specialized verification capability that these tools lack" (§4.1 para 2, p. 7) — and read their contribution as filling a specific gap in the scholarly toolkit (§5 para 2, p. 8). They think the result follows from the cascading architecture and cross-source author verification, which they argue raises recall while multi-source confirmation holds precision (§3 para 2, p. 3). The implications they draw are that verification can now run at the speed of content production (§5 para 2, p. 8), with use cases for authors validating before submission, researchers checking LLM-suggested citations, reviewers auditing manuscripts, publishers gating submission pipelines, and bibliometricians filtering datasets (§5 para 1, pp. 7-8). The practical value they claim is unlimited free verification, automatic LaTeX filtering and BibTeX export (§4.1 para 2, p. 7; §5 para 2, p. 8), plus the elimination of installation barriers claimed for the web delivery itself (§3 para 1, p. 3). Limitations they state: none — there is no limitations section and no limitation of the tool is acknowledged anywhere. Future research they discuss: none, and no next step is named. The section headed Conclusions does reiterate the significance of the problem and summarise the design, and it has no results or evaluation to summarise; it opens with a page of prospective use cases instead. There is no acknowledgements section for people who helped without making a significant contribution, and no funding statement of any kind.
