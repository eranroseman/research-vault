## 2. Summary

- **Problem.** To give researchers, reviewers and publishers an immediate, free way to check whether a reference corresponds to a real work, (Sec 1, para 1, p. 1), motivated by LLM "reference hallucination" (Abstract, p. 1; Sec 1, para 3, p. 1). No hypothesis or research question is stated; the objective is the tool. Sec 2 (pp. 2–3) frames the need: pre-LLM error rates of 25–54 %; LLM fabrications as systematic rather than random, at rates of 6 % to over 30 % (Agrawal et al. 2024); generation now nearly free while verification is not.
- **Method.** Paper type: systems/tool paper (`references/method-menu.md`, calibration table, "Systems" row). No method family fits and there is no evaluation of any family, so the paper can show what the tool is designed to do, not how well it does it. A React/TypeScript web application in four modules — LaTeX-command filtering, BibTeX parsing, multi-source search, presentation/export (Sec 3, para 1, p. 3). Sources: CrossRef primary, Semantic Scholar fallback, OpenAlex completing the cascade (Sec 3, para 2, p. 3). Procedure (Algorithm 1, p. 4; Figure 1, p. 5): strip LaTeX; fetch three CrossRef candidates, or Semantic Scholar candidates if none (lines 1–5); score the best and detect issues (lines 6–7); if the score is below 70 or an issue exists, query Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex, intersect the three author lists, add +10 and merge metadata when at least two authors are confirmed, and flag authors outside the intersection as potentially fabricated (lines 8–29); compute confidence, generate APA and BibTeX, return `exists` as bestScore > 50 (lines 30–32). Similarity is normalised Levenshtein on lower-cased alphanumeric strings (Eq. 1, p. 6); author matching checks family names from the primary source against the query, and capitalised query tokens matching nothing count as possible fabricated authors (Sec 3, para 7, p. 6). Confidence: Eq. 2 when title similarity exceeds 80 % and author similarity is below 90 %; Eq. 3, the mean of four field similarities plus a bonus in [0, 10], for structured input with high matching; penalties of −20 for title and author mismatches and −10 to −20 for journal discrepancies and each fake author (Sec 3, para 8, p. 6). Footnote 2 (p. 6) says thresholds and penalties were calibrated empirically against known hallucinations. Features (Sec 4, pp. 6–7): quick check of one free-form reference; batch check of BibTeX or newline-separated lists at 800 ms intervals; APA 7th edition and BibTeX output built from the database record; download and clipboard export. Table 1 (Sec 4.1, p. 7) compares 13 features (derived) across five tools. No participants, materials, pilot or sample are involved.
- **Results.** No results section and no measurement of any kind. The only numbers are design constants: three candidates; thresholds of 70 (fallback), 80 (Figure 1 "Verified") and 50 (`exists`); the +10 bonus; the penalty ranges; the 80 % and 90 % cut-offs; the 800 ms interval. Table 1 is a feature checklist, not data.
- **Discussion.** The authors' interpretation is confined to Sec 5 (pp. 7–8): five use cases — authors, LLM users, reviewers, publishers' submission pipelines, bibliometricians filtering spurious references — then a restatement that the tool complements reference managers and lets researchers "validate citations at the speed of modern content production". No limitations; no future work; the conclusion summarises the system and reiterates significance but has no methods or results to summarise. No acknowledgements, funding or conflict-of-interest statement. An Availability section (p. 8) gives the MIT licence and the URL.

## 3. Discussion

### Importance

The problem is real and timely, but the contribution is an engineering assembly whose title promises hallucination detection while the mechanism only tests presence in three metadata indexes.

- **Location**: Title, p. 1; Algorithm 1, line 32; Sec 3, p. 3, para 2. **Observation**: The title reads "Detecting Citation Hallucinations", yet the returned `exists` flag is `bestScore > 50` from string matching, and Sec 3 concedes that "no individual scholarly database achieves complete coverage of the academic literature". **Evidence or criterion**: N14. **Why it matters**: A non-match shows absence from three indexes, not fabrication.
- **Location**: Sec 1, p. 2, para 3 (the paragraph beginning "The rapid adoption"); References, p. 9. **Observation**: The urgency case rests on a Fortune news article (Goldman, 2026) plus an ICLR count and a "24.52%" acceptance figure that carry no citation. **Evidence or criterion**: N20; References, p. 9. **Why it matters**: The problem is plausible but its documented scale is second-hand.
- **Location**: Sec 4.1, p. 7, para 2. **Observation**: The paper frames the tool as complementary ("It does not seek to replace comprehensive tools like Zotero"); its distinguishing claim is "unlimited free verification". **Evidence or criterion**: N3. **Why it matters**: The significance is access, not method.

### Credibility

The tool plausibly exists, but this version-1 arXiv preprint (p. 1 stamp, no venue named) supports none of its performance claims, and its pseudocode, flowchart and equations contradict one another, so it is neither evidence of effectiveness nor a usable specification.

- **Location**: Sec 3, p. 3, para 2; Sec 3, p. 6, para 7; footnote 2, p. 6. **Observation**: Three empirical claims — "achieves higher recall than any single-source approach while maintaining precision through multi-source confirmation"; "particularly effective at detecting LLM hallucinations"; "empirically calibrated" against "known hallucinations" — with no test set, precision, recall or calibration data. **Evidence or criterion**: N1, N2; quantitative-results: claim–evidence mismatch. **Why it matters**: The only claims about how well the tool works are asserted, not shown; this is the major flaw.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 23 and 31. **Observation**: `correctedMetadata` is assigned only at line 23, inside the fallback branch (line 8) and the `|confirmedAuthors| ≥ 2` test (line 21), yet line 31 generates the outputs from it unconditionally, so on the ordinary path (score 70 or more, no issues) it is unassigned. **Evidence or criterion**: C4. **Why it matters**: Demonstrated inconsistency, not missing reporting: the pseudocode cannot be implemented as printed.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 8, 30, 32; Figure 1, p. 5. **Observation**: Existence is decided on `bestScore > 50`, the fallback fires below 70, Figure 1 splits Verified from Partial Match at 80, and the penalties applied at line 30 produce `confidence` without touching `bestScore`. **Evidence or criterion**: C2, C7. **Why it matters**: A reader cannot tell which quantity the "exists" verdict reflects, or whether a detected fake author can change it.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 3–4, 10, 14, 18–19; Sec 3, p. 6, para 7. **Observation**: When CrossRef returns nothing, `bestMatch` is a Semantic Scholar record (line 4), line 10 queries Semantic Scholar again and line 14 labels its authors `crossRefAuthors`, so the line 18 intersection has at most two independent sources. Line 19 defines suspects as union minus intersection, so an author present in two of three sources is a suspect, whereas the prose flags "authors appearing in only one source"; the query's own authors never enter line 19. **Evidence or criterion**: Algorithm 1, lines 4, 10, 14, 19; Sec 3, p. 6, para 7. **Why it matters**: The multi-source guarantee is weakest where invoked, and prose and pseudocode disagree on what triggers the tool's most consequential label.

Internal-validity threats and demand characteristics do not apply: no experiment or participants are reported.

### Novelty

The combination of three public metadata APIs, normalized Levenshtein and author-list intersection is sensible but unremarkable, and the "gap" claim is broader than the literature the paper cites can support.

- **Location**: Table 1, p. 7; Sec 4.1, p. 7, para 2. **Observation**: The only comparison is with four reference managers the paper says it does not compete with; in six of thirteen rows (derived count) CheckIfExist is the sole tick. Commercial services and prior academic checkers are absent. **Evidence or criterion**: N3, N25; source-types: novelty claims broader than the cited literature supports are a claim–evidence mismatch. **Why it matters**: Table 1 shows a category difference, not an advance over tools that do the same job.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 18–19; Sec 3, p. 6, para 7; Eq. 2 and Eq. 3, p. 6. **Observation**: The most specific ideas are cross-source author intersection, the heuristic flagging "capitalized tokens in the query that match neither title words, journal name, year, nor any real author family name", and a hand-set scoring recipe. **Evidence or criterion**: N8, N11. **Why it matters**: Plausible and untested; the heuristic is undefined for lower-case or all-caps input and the recipe is incomplete.

### Applicability

Usable now as a free quick check for journal and conference references, provided a negative is read as absence from three indexes rather than as fabrication.

- **Location**: Footnote 1, p. 1; Availability, p. 8; Sec 4, p. 6, para 2 and p. 7, para 3; Algorithm 1, line 32. **Observation**: A public URL, an MIT licence, batch BibTeX input and export of corrected BibTeX are described; no repository or version is given, no error rate is reported, and the 50-point threshold is unexplained. **Evidence or criterion**: N1, N15. **Why it matters**: The hosted tool can drift unnoticed, and a user cannot know how often a flag is wrong.
- **Location**: Sec 3, p. 3, para 2; Sec 3, p. 6, para 6; Figure 1, p. 5. **Observation**: Matching is title-string based; DOIs or arXiv identifiers are never mentioned as inputs; books, theses, reports and datasets are not discussed, although the paper admits CrossRef "may lack coverage of preprints, regional journals, or older publications". **Evidence or criterion**: N13, N14. **Why it matters**: Humanities, social-science and standards-heavy bibliographies will yield many non-matches that are not hallucinations.

### Generalizability

There are no results to generalize; the design's reach is bounded by three metadata indexes and by name conventions the paper never specifies.

- **Location**: Sec 3, p. 3, para 2; Sec 3, p. 6, paras 6–7; Eq. 1, p. 6. **Observation**: The paper's own caveats ("preprints, regional journals, or older publications") mark where it will under-perform; normalization is "conversion to lowercase and removal of non-alphanumeric characters", and family-name matching across initials, diacritics, particles and non-Latin scripts is not described. **Evidence or criterion**: N11, N14; experiment-design: external validity — to whom, when, where. **Why it matters**: Non-English, older and non-journal literature is where non-match and hallucination diverge most.
- **Location**: Footnote 2, p. 6; Sec 1, p. 2, para 3 ("The rapid adoption"). **Observation**: Thresholds were calibrated on "known hallucinations" of unstated origin and domain; the "subtle alterations" the paper itself describes — expanded initials, paraphrased titles — are never revisited against the 80% title rule. **Evidence or criterion**: N2, N28. **Why it matters**: Whether the thresholds transfer across fields, or catch subtler fabrications, is unassessable.

### Scalability

Adequate for a short bibliography, but the sequential 800 ms loop and up to four API calls per entry make the large-scale uses proposed in the Conclusions unrealistic; no throughput or cost figures are given.

- **Location**: Sec 4, p. 7, para 2 (the paragraph beginning "For comprehensive bibliography audits"); Algorithm 1, lines 2, 4, 10, 11; Sec 5, p. 8, para 1. **Observation**: Entries are processed "sequentially with rate limiting (800ms intervals)" and one reference can trigger up to four external requests (derived); a 100-entry bibliography then waits at least 80 s and a 1,000-entry set at least 800 s, about 13 min (derived), before any API latency; rate-limit or outage handling is not stated. Yet "large-scale bibliometric analyses" and publisher pipelines are proposed with no cost or batch limit discussed. **Evidence or criterion**: N4, N17, N19. **Why it matters**: The "within seconds" claim holds per reference, not per bibliography; the proposed uses need an architecture the paper does not present.

### Assumptions

Three load-bearing assumptions — absence from three indexes implies fabrication, cross-source author lists are complete and comparable, and hand-set thresholds transfer — are neither stated nor examined, and the key variables are defined inconsistently.

- **Location**: Algorithm 1, line 32; Sec 3, p. 3, para 2; Sec 5, p. 8, para 1. **Observation**: `exists` is a score threshold, the paper admits incomplete coverage, and the Conclusions still propose filtering "potentially spurious references from datasets". **Evidence or criterion**: N14. **Why it matters**: Applied as proposed, the assumption would delete real but unindexed works from bibliometric datasets.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 18–21; Sec 3, p. 6, para 7. **Observation**: Confirmation needs two authors present in all three sources (line 21); a missing record, a truncated author list or a single-author work makes it impossible and, via line 19, puts every author in `suspectAuthors`. **Evidence or criterion**: N10; Algorithm 1, line 19. **Why it matters**: The failure mode points the fabrication label at real people.
- **Location**: Footnote 2, p. 6; Eq. 3, p. 6; Algorithm 1, line 8. **Observation**: The Eq. 3 bonus is "awarded when author information is validated across multiple databases", but that validation runs only when the score is below 70 or issues exist, so under "high matching across all fields" the bonus is unreachable. **Evidence or criterion**: C6. **Why it matters**: The calibration claim covers a rule the pseudocode cannot execute as written.
- **Location**: Abstract, p. 1; Sec 1, p. 1, para 3; Eq. 1, Eq. 2, Eq. 3, p. 6; Algorithm 1, line 30. **Observation**: "Hallucination" gets one Abstract clause and one Sec 1, para 3 sentence, then examples; "confidence" by Eq. 2 for one regime, Eq. 3 for another and an unspecified ComputeFinalScore for the rest; Eq. 1 yields [0, 1] while every threshold is on a 0–100 scale. **Evidence or criterion**: N8, N9, N23, C10. **Why it matters**: The output a user sees cannot be traced to one definition. No parametric tests are used.

### Readability

The narrative sections are clear, but the technical core is hard to pin down because pseudocode, flowchart and equations describe three different procedures and leave symbols undefined.

- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 3–5, 10–11 and 26–27; Figure 1, p. 5; Sec 3, p. 3, para 2. **Observation**: The flowchart has no counterpart to the empty-CrossRef branch (lines 3–5); it routes an author "Mismatch" to "Corrected Citation" where the pseudocode records an issue; the prose calls Semantic Scholar "a fallback source" while lines 10–11 query both fallbacks unconditionally. **Evidence or criterion**: C3, C8, C9. **Why it matters**: Whichever artefact a reader trusts, one of the other two contradicts it.
- **Location**: Figure 1, p. 5. **Observation**: Every path — Verified, Partial Match, Mismatch — terminates at "Generate APA + BibTeX"; no node represents a non-match or a hallucination; there is no legend. **Evidence or criterion**: Figure 1, p. 5. **Why it matters**: The figure never shows how the paper's headline output, a detected hallucination, is reported.
- **Location**: Eq. 3, p. 6; Algorithm 1, Require line and line 32. **Observation**: S_journal and S_year are never defined; the input `E` and the returned `sources` are never used. **Evidence or criterion**: N7, N24, N26. **Why it matters**: A reader cannot reimplement from the paper; the prose itself is scholarly and culturally neutral.

### Ethics

The goal is benign and the tool is free, but the paper is silent on two foreseeable harms — false "fabricated author" flags aimed at real people, and confidential manuscript references sent to third-party services — and carries no funding or conflict statement.

- **Location**: Algorithm 1, line 27; Sec 5, p. 8, paras 1–2. **Observation**: The tool emits "Potential fabricated authors: "; the Conclusions recommend it to reviewers for "identifying potential fabrications warranting author clarification" and to publishers for manuscripts "flagged by AI detection tools"; false-positive rates are not discussed; the only mitigation offered is "tools that support human judgment". **Evidence or criterion**: N1, N18. **Why it matters**: A flag with unknown error rate, presented as a fabrication signal, can attach an integrity accusation to an innocent author.
- **Location**: Algorithm 1, lines 2, 10, 11; Sec 4, p. 7, para 2; Sec 5, p. 8, para 1. **Observation**: Reviewers of unpublished manuscripts are a named use case and each reference goes to up to three external APIs; whether inputs are logged or retained is not stated. **Evidence or criterion**: N16. **Why it matters**: Using the tool as recommended may breach reviewer confidentiality.
- **Location**: Availability, p. 8; footnote 2, p. 6; Abstract, p. 1; References, pp. 8–9. **Observation**: MIT licence and public URL are positives. Gaps, described neutrally: no funding, conflict-of-interest or acknowledgement statement; footnote 2's calibration data are not shared; the reference list has volume/year conflicts; the Abstract places the ICLR cases among papers "accepted at premier machine learning conferences" while the Introduction says they were "identified before acceptance". **Evidence or criterion**: N15, N18, C1, C12, C13. **Why it matters**: None of this is evidence of misconduct; together it is a transparency gap to weigh.

No human subjects were involved (N19), so IRB, consent and deception items do not apply.

### Inconsistency list (stage 2)

C1. Abstract: "AI-hallucinated citations even in papers accepted at premier machine learning conferences such as NeurIPS and ICLR"; Sec 1, para 3 (p. 2): the ICLR citations were in "papers under review ... identified before acceptance". Whether ICLR papers were accepted differs.
C2. Decision thresholds: Algorithm 1 line 32 returns exists = bestScore > 50; Figure 1 (p. 5) uses "Score > 70%?" to trigger fallback and "Score > 80%?" to separate Verified from Partial Match; Sec 3 prose (p. 6) mentions neither a 50 nor an 80 threshold on the composite score. The 50 appears only at line 32; the composite-score 80 appears only in Figure 1.
C3. Algorithm 1 lines 3–5 (empty CrossRef result → Semantic Scholar supplies the candidates) has no counterpart in Figure 1, where Semantic Scholar appears only in the post-scoring fallback branch.
C4. Algorithm 1 assigns correctedMetadata only at line 23, inside the fallback branch (line 8) and inside the |confirmedAuthors| ≥ 2 test (line 21), yet line 31 uses it unconditionally. On the path bestScore ≥ 70 with no issues — the ordinary "verified" path — the variable that generates the APA and BibTeX outputs is never assigned. Likewise `sources` (line 32) is never assigned.
C5. Algorithm 1 line 22 adds a flat +10; Eq. 3 (p. 6) states β_ms ∈ [0, 10], a range.
C6. Eq. 3 (p. 6) applies "for structured input with high matching across all fields" and includes β_ms "awarded when author information is validated across multiple databases"; Algorithm 1 line 8 performs that multi-database validation only when bestScore < 70 or issues exist. Under high matching with no issues the bonus can never be awarded, so the two locations describe incompatible conditions.
C7. Sec 3, para 8, p. 6: penalties for title/author/journal mismatches and fake authors are part of "the composite confidence score"; Algorithm 1 line 32 decides `exists` on bestScore, which those penalties (applied at line 30 to produce `confidence`) do not affect. The prose describes one composite score; the pseudocode uses two different quantities for existence and confidence.
C8. Figure 1: "Cross-validate Authors" → "Mismatch" → "Corrected Citation" → "Generate APA + BibTeX", i.e. an author mismatch yields a corrected citation. Algorithm 1 lines 21–23 produce corrected metadata only on confirmation (≥ 2 confirmed authors); lines 26–27 turn a mismatch into an issue, not a correction.
C9. Sec 3, para 2 (p. 3) describes Semantic Scholar as "a fallback source" and OpenAlex as completing "the validation cascade", i.e. sequential; Algorithm 1 lines 10–11 query both unconditionally once the fallback fires.
C10. Score scale: Eq. 1 (p. 6) yields a similarity in [0, 1]; Eq. 2 and Eq. 3 (p. 6), Algorithm 1 lines 8, 22, 32 and Figure 1 treat scores on a 0–100 (or percentage) scale with no conversion stated.
