# Requirements: sourcing (the build-or-buy phase)

> **For agentic workers:** this document consumes an **approved** requirement set and produces build-or-buy verdicts in its own artifact. It is one of two successors `writing-reqs` offers at its review gate; the other is `writing-specs`.
>
> **Status: NOT ELICITED.** This set was not produced by the process `writing-reqs` specifies. Nobody was interviewed. Every requirement below is either a decision the author made explicitly while specifying `writing-reqs` on 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-02, or a method this project actually ran and can point at. **Anything that was neither is an open question, not a requirement.** The honest next step is to run `writing-reqs` on this need and replace this document with its output.
>
> **Identifiers are `S`, not `R`,** and are never changed or reused. A red-team pass on the sibling document found INCOSE's own rule numbers sitting in a column of that set's identifiers, meaning two things at once; separate namespaces prevent it here.

## Problem

Deciding whether to build or adopt is a distinct act from deciding what is needed, and it was running inside `writing-reqs` as a third phase. Three things made that wrong. It has other callers — a build-or-buy question arises without a requirements interview at all, and #75's disposition survey was exactly that. It runs on a different clock — requirements settle once, sourcing re-runs whenever a requirement's meaning narrows. And its verdicts were landing inside an artifact that had already been approved, changing the thing after agreement.

## Solution

A skill that takes an approved requirement set, screens candidates against it, and records verdicts in its own artifact. It never edits the requirement set.

## Assumptions

- **Candidates are readable.** Screening judges the artifact body — a skill file, a template, a licence — so a candidate whose body cannot be obtained cannot be screened. Nobody is obliged to publish one.

## Prior art

Read 2026-09-02 from primaries already held, after the author named the process — problem, solution, assumptions, prior art, requirements, competitive analysis, update. **This is a first pass over two standards, not a survey.** No competitive analysis has run.

**ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017, 6.1.1 (Acquisition process).** Make-or-buy is a trade-off, not a screen:

> *"The Decision Management process and System Analysis process are often used to support trade-offs for the acquisition strategy. Examples include: **determining a make or buy decision**, as well as the suitability of specific COTS or modified OTS solutions and supplier evaluation."*

And for the case that actually applies here — free and open-source components — it names what acquisition reduces to:

> *"If the strategy calls for acquisition of specific commercial-off-the-shelf software or open source software, acquisition can be limited to identifying the supplier, accepting or negotiating the conditions in a **pre-defined licence**… determining **rights to intellectual property and data rights** in the software system, and agreeing on the price."*

S10 required reading the licence. This adds two things it does not carry: identifying who the supplier is, and intellectual-property and data rights as distinct from the licence grant. Applied as S13.

**ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024.** Adopting does not excuse the analysis:

> *"**Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace** system feasibility analyses or the risk reduction analyses and effectiveness assessments needed to confirm that **interfaces are compatible**. This off-the-shelf approach can reduce the need to go through earlier decision gates, but it does not eliminate the analysis necessary to reduce risks."*

This is the strongest finding for this set, because "install as-is" is the top rung of the ladder and the one most likely to be taken as a shortcut. Applied as S11.

Same document, on recording:

> *"'Record key design decisions and the rationale' has been added to ensure to keep the decisions and their **rationale retrievable and accountable**."*

Applied as S12. The screen this project ran recorded verdicts; it recorded reasons unevenly, and two verdicts were later found to rest on grounds absent from the artifact.

### The finding that locates the split, and the test for it

ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 **removed** the separate category buy-options used to have:

> *"The term 'Candidate Non-Developmental-Items (NDI)' including COTS (commercial-off-the-shelf), reuse of a previous design or acquirer provided items for obtaining system elements **has not been used and these have been included into 'system design alternatives'**."*

Read first as an objection to splitting sourcing out. It is not. What 15288 removes buy-options *from* is the requirements phase, and where it puts them is design — which is what this split did: sourcing no longer runs inside `writing-reqs`, and its verdicts no longer land in a requirement set.

The residual difference is smaller than it looked. 15288 has design generate and evaluate buy-options among its alternatives; here sourcing screens them first and design receives the result. Those converge **provided the design phase gets both artifacts**, which is why S14 exists. Sourcing pre-computes an analysis rather than removing it from design's reach, and S12's rationale requirement is what lets design overturn a verdict rather than inherit it.

**This is testable, and the test is already scheduled.** The first real run feeds `writing-reqs`' requirement set and its sourcing verdicts to `writing-specs` together. If `writing-specs` can design from the pair, the arrangement holds. If it needs to re-open a screened candidate, the pre-screening cost something and belongs inside design after all.

## Requirements

Each requirement names the ruling or the run it comes from. Nothing here is inferred from a standard that was not read.

### Input and ordering

**S1** — Consumes a requirement set that has been approved, and refuses one that has not.
*Fit:* the run reads the set's recorded verdict (`writing-reqs` R32) and stops when it is anything but confirmed.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 — "Sourcing consumes reqs, and reqs must be approved before sourcing begins."

**S2** — Re-runs when a requirement's meaning changes, and voids the verdicts scored against the old meaning.
*Fit:* every verdict records which version of which requirement it was scored against; a narrowing recorded under `writing-reqs` R19 marks each affected verdict void rather than stale.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 — "lets finalize reqs and then we redo all the sourcing" — plus R19's amendment, which was itself forced by R22 and R4 narrowing without retiring.

### Verdicts

**S3** — Verdicts live in this skill's own artifact, never in the requirement set.
*Fit:* the requirement set is byte-identical before and after a run.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 (Q19). Keeping them in the requirement set would change an artifact after it was approved, which is what approval-before-sourcing exists to prevent.

**S4** — A verdict names a component, never a whole skill.
*Fit:* no verdict rejects or adopts a body wholesale; each names the part it judges.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-01 — "the fact that a skill violates one requirement doesn't mean it has another part that fulfils another requirement better than any other skill." Applying it moved `requirements-clarity` from Reject to Adapt: its 90/100 score gate was declined and its decline signals taken.

**S5** — A verdict carries one of six treatments: install as-is, fork and keep merging, copy frozen, copy plus a delta, author to someone else's design and credit it, or write from scratch.
*Fit:* every verdict names exactly one, and a verdict naming none fails.
*Source:* the author's rulings, 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-01 — "the best option is to find a plugin that has all the skills we need that we can install and use as is", "less changes is better, homebrew is last resource", and "there should be 'copy plus delta', it isn't finalized". The sixth was added in conversation and is not in the drafted ladder ADR, which remains a proposal.

**S6** — Finding nothing available produces a decision to build, and no change to the requirements.
*Fit:* a run returning no adoptable component emits a build verdict and edits nothing upstream.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 — "if you can't source anything, then you develop." An earlier version imported FAR 10.002(c)'s re-evaluation clause, which exists because government procurement has a statutory preference for commercial items; no such preference operates here, so a null result is evidence about the world rather than about the requirements.

### Method

**S7** — Screens each candidate in isolation, without knowledge of the others.
*Fit:* no screening run can name a candidate it was not given, rank candidates, or recommend one.
*Source:* the method this project ran on 2026-09-01, recorded in the sourcing screen. Twelve candidates, one isolated agent each.

**S8** — Judges the artifact body, never a description, README or roster position.
*Fit:* every verdict quotes the body it judged.
*Source:* the same run, and the failure it was correcting: an earlier pass read a candidate's exit path off its roster rather than its body, and was wrong about all three candidates it checked.

**S9** — Records what was read and the version it was read at, and treats an unverifiable provenance claim as a failed screen.
*Fit:* every verdict names a retrievable version, and a verdict whose cited text is absent from that version is void.
*Source:* a measured failure, not a precaution. The 2026-09-01 screen recorded a single date for all twelve candidates; re-reading spec-kit the same day found neither behaviour the rejection cited, so at least one screen read something other than what the note claims.

**S10** — Reads licences from the licence file, not from a platform's detected label.
*Fit:* every verdict names the file it read the licence from, or records that none exists.
*Source:* the same run — `framing-doc` was found to have no LICENSE file at all, verified rather than inferred.

**S11** — A verdict to adopt names the compatibility check it ran, not only the requirements the candidate passed.
*Fit:* every adopt or adapt verdict states what it checked at the seam — invocation, inputs, outputs, and the harnesses under `writing-reqs` R34 — and a verdict claiming a pass with no seam check fails.
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024 — *"Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace system feasibility analyses or the risk reduction analyses and effectiveness assessments needed to confirm that interfaces are compatible."* Read 2026-09-02. This project's own screen measured requirement coverage and never checked a seam.

**S12** — A verdict carries its rationale, not only its outcome.
*Fit:* a reader can say why each verdict went the way it did without asking, and a verdict whose stated grounds are absent from the artifact it judged is void.
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-2:2024 on 15288:2023 6.4.5.3(d)(3) — rationale kept *"retrievable and accountable"*. Read 2026-09-02. Forced by measurement, not preference: the 2026-09-01 screen rejected spec-kit `/specify` on two behaviours that re-reading the same day found in neither current preset.

**S13** — Records the supplier, the licence, and the intellectual-property and data rights, as three separate findings.
*Fit:* each is named per candidate, or recorded as absent; "MIT" alone does not satisfy it.
*Source:* ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017 6.1.1 — for COTS and open-source software, acquisition *"can be limited to identifying the supplier, accepting or negotiating the conditions in a pre-defined licence… determining rights to intellectual property and data rights."* Read 2026-09-02. S10 covered only the licence.

**S14** — Hands both artifacts to `writing-specs`: the approved requirement set it consumed, and its own verdicts.
*Fit:* the design phase can reach every verdict and its rationale without re-running the screen, and can overturn one — a run that hands over verdicts as settled rather than as evidence fails.
*Source:* the author's ruling, 2026-09-02 — "feeding it to it together with the reqs will achieve the same results". It is what makes this arrangement converge with ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023, which evaluates buy-options among design alternatives rather than ahead of them; pre-screening is only equivalent if design still sees the alternatives and the reasons.

## Constraints

- Runs on Claude Code and Codex, like its sibling.
- Consumes `writing-reqs`' artifact and produces its own; it does not modify its input.
- Its own successor is `writing-specs`, which receives both artifacts.

## Open questions

These were not elicited and must not be treated as settled.

- **What is the artifact's shape?** `writing-reqs` has a specified output; this skill's is undefined. Nothing here says what a sourcing artifact contains beyond verdicts.
- **Does a near-miss feed back?** Where a candidate almost fits and relaxing one requirement would admit it, FAR 10.002(c) — *"determine whether the need can be restated to permit commercial products"* — describes exactly that case, and it is the half of the clause that may transfer where the null case does not. Unresolved: whether this skill may propose a relaxation, and if so how it returns to an approved set.
- **What is "actively maintained" worth?** The author's ruling — "the actively maintained is a quality mark. I don't use verbatim a section from a skill with 1 user" — is recorded but has no requirement, because no threshold was elicited.
- **Has enough prior art been read?** A first pass over 12207 and 24748-2 ran on 2026-09-02 and produced three requirements. Nothing else was searched — no COTS-selection literature, no procurement practice beyond what the sibling document already holds, and **no competitive analysis at all**, which the process places next.
- **Does this skill screen its own candidates?** Nothing says whether sourcing can be sourced.
