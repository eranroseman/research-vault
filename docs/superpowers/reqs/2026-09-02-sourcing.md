# Requirements: sourcing

> **What it does.** Takes a requirement set, searches for existing components that might serve it, and reports what each one covers. It decides nothing.
>
> **Written fresh 2026-09-03.** The previous set — 20 requirements, accreted over two days — is evidence, not a draft to edit: it carried a screening stage that the design phase owns, and most of its late churn was reconciling rules against a stage that should not have been there. Git holds it at `68fa8a0`. Requirements that survive keep their identifiers; the rest are retired and never reused.
>
> **Status: derived, not elicited.** Nobody was interviewed for this. Every requirement traces to a ruling the author made while specifying the sibling skill, a failure this project measured, or prior art read from a primary.

## Problem

Deciding what to build requires knowing what already exists, and that question is asked at a different time, on a different clock, and often for a different reason than the requirements were written. Left inside the requirements phase it distorts both: build-or-buy verdicts land in an artifact that was already agreed, and the search gets scoped to one need when it has others.

## Solution

A skill between requirements and design. It searches on what the requirements say is essential, reports what it found and how it looked, and describes each candidate against the whole requirement set. The design phase screens, chooses and decides; this one supplies the alternatives and the evidence about them.

## Assumptions

- **Candidates are readable.** A finding rests on the candidate's own text, so a candidate whose body cannot be obtained cannot be described. Nobody is obliged to publish one.

## Prior art

Read 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-03: ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 §6.1.1 and 24748-2 on acquisition; five corpora for the competitive analysis — COTS and open-source selection methods (OTSO, PORE, MiHOS, CAP), open-source health metrics, procurement's participation gate, supply-chain dependency review, and design-alternative evaluation. Full record: `docs/research/`.

**Two findings shaped more than one requirement.** Make-or-buy is a trade-off supported by decision analysis, ending in an estimate rather than a verdict (12207 §6.1.1) — which is why this skill gathers and hands on. And ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 folds candidate off-the-shelf items into *"system design alternatives"*, evaluated inside design — which is why the screening stage this document used to carry now belongs downstream.

**One null is load-bearing.** All five corpora were asked for a rule of the form *"screen in if it meets N of M requirements"*. None publishes one. What they do instead is search on a designated subset and record everything else rather than gate on it.

## Requirements

**Retired, never reused: S7, S10, S15, S17, S18, S20, S21, S23.** The screening rules (S15, S17, S18, S21, S23) moved to `writing-specs` as inherited requirements. S7, S10 and S20 merged into S5, S13 and S24.

### Reading the requirements

**S1** — Reads the requirement set, and asks about anything it needs and cannot find.
*Fit:* it needs which requirements are must-have, since S19 searches on those, and a version per requirement, since S2 depends on it. Where either is missing or unclear it asks before searching. It never refuses the work and never guesses.
*Why:* a search aimed at a guessed must-have list looks like a search and is not one, and nothing downstream can tell the difference.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* the author's rulings of 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-03.

### Searching

**S19** — Searches on the must-have requirements only, from more than one source, until new sources return only candidates already seen.
*Fit:* the target is the must-haves, never the whole set. Where results are too many to report, the run searches again adding the remaining requirements and says so — that narrows the search, never the candidates.
*Why:* searching on everything narrows the field before anything has been read, which discards candidates for requirements they were never meant to satisfy. Saturation, not a count, is what says the search is done.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* the author's ruling of 2026-09-03; OTSO's stopping rule — *"a simple strategy for ending the search is to use several sources"*.

**S24** — Declares the search before running it, and reports what it actually did.
*Fit:* declared in advance — the candidate concept, the kinds of source, the stopping rule. Reported after — every source reached, the terms used on each, where saturation fell, and any source or query added mid-search **with its reason**. Where a source returns nothing, the run says how it established the source and the query were working.
*Why:* a null from a search whose method is unrecorded is not a finding about the field, and additions made after an unwelcome result are invisible unless declared.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* two measured failures in this project — a null that was trustworthy only because a control query happened to be run, and six queries of which three were added after the first three came back empty.

### Describing each candidate

**S16** — Records, per candidate, which requirements it covers, which it does not, and which could not be determined.
*Fit:* three states, and *undetermined* is the default. The run states affirmatively where a candidate was fully assessed; absent that, coverage reads as incomplete rather than absent.
*Why:* this is the product. Everything else describes how it was made.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* NTIA's SBOM minimum elements, which makes unknown the default and puts the burden on the recorder to claim completeness.

**S4** — Describes the smallest part of a candidate that could be taken without taking the rest.
*Fit:* the unit follows the candidate's kind — a section, rule or step in a skill; a module or exported interface in a library; a clause in a standard. A finding may describe a whole candidate where the whole is that smallest part.
*Why:* a body dismissed whole takes its usable parts with it, and getting them back costs a second pass. This project paid that three times.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* the author's ruling of 2026-09-01; PORE and MiHOS both record at *(one requirement, one feature)*.

**S8** — Grounds every finding in the candidate's body.
*Fit:* two steps. A description, README or registry entry may narrow the **search**; only the body grounds a **finding**. A candidate whose body was never read is recorded as not-examined, never as covering or not covering.
*Why:* an earlier pass in this project read three candidates' behaviour off their roster position and was wrong about all three.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* that failure; the author's two-step framing of 2026-09-03.

**S9** — Quotes text present in the candidate at the version named.
*Fit:* a mechanical check **flags** a mismatch; only a read confirming the text is absent voids the finding. Where the read finds it present, the run records what the check tripped on.
*Why:* this project rejected a candidate on two behaviours absent from its artifact — and separately produced three verbatim quotes that failed mechanical checks on a soft hyphen, an HTML entity, and spacing. Both failures are real and they pull in opposite directions.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* both failures; HALLMARK, on citation verifiers over-flagging.

**S12** — Carries the reasoning behind each finding, not only its result.
*Fit:* a reader can say why a finding went the way it did without asking. A finding whose stated grounds are absent from the artifact it describes is void.
*Why:* design can overturn a finding only if it can see the reasoning; without it, findings arrive as verdicts, which is what this skill exists not to produce.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* 24748-2 on 15288 §6.4.5.3(d)(3) — rationale kept *"retrievable and accountable"*.

**S13** — Records the supplier and the originator where they differ, the licence, and the intellectual-property assertions.
*Fit:* due when a candidate is described, not when it is found. The licence is read from the candidate's own files — a licence file, a README, a manifest, a header — and the run names which it read. **No licence file is not no licence**: "none found" is sayable only after looking, and only with the places named.
*Why:* a candidate's own terms bound what can be done with it regardless of how well it fits, and a licence nobody looked for is not a licence nobody has.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* 12207 §6.1.1 on open-source acquisition; SPDX, which takes the declared licence from *the package's own files* and bars a third-party repository's label from that field.

**S11** — Records what the body shows about fitting: inputs, outputs, how it is invoked, and which harnesses it runs on.
*Fit:* what a reading of the body establishes. Actually exercising a candidate is design's work, and this requirement does not ask for it.
*Why:* a coverage table says a candidate could serve a requirement; it does not say whether it could be reached from here.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* 24748-2 — *"Use of off-the shelf system elements does not replace… the analyses needed to confirm that interfaces are compatible."*

**S22** — Records capability the requirement set did not ask for.
*Fit:* recorded as its own finding with its expected effect — helpful, neutral, or a cost. A candidate carrying twice the surface area needed is not thereby better.
*Why:* for one developer, unasked-for capability is surface to understand and drift to track.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* MiHOS, which splits *OverMatch* from *Surplus* and prices both.

### What it does not do

**S5** — Describes each candidate on its own, and decides nothing.
*Fit:* each candidate is described against the requirements alone, never against another candidate: no ranking, no shortlist, no single answer. A per-candidate opinion **is** permitted and is evidence — the run may say which treatment a candidate's evidence points toward, for every candidate or for none. What it may not do is close the decision.
*Why:* screening, choosing and rejecting are the design phase's, which sees the requirements and these findings together. A skill that decides gives design a conclusion to accept rather than alternatives to weigh.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 3 · *Source:* the author's rulings of 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-03; 15288:2023, which evaluates off-the-shelf items among design alternatives; FAR 10.002(d), which leaves the choice with the contracting officer downstream of the research.

**S6** — Reports when the search found nothing that covers a must-have.
*Fit:* stated as a result with the coverage that was measured, editing nothing upstream and naming no decision.
*Why:* a null is a finding about the field and an input to someone else's decision. What follows from it is not this skill's call.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* the author's rulings of 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-03; FAR 10.002(c) and (d)(2).

**S3** — Puts its findings in its own artifact and leaves the requirement set untouched.
*Fit:* the requirement set is byte-identical before and after a run, and the run names the path of the artifact it wrote.
*Why:* the requirements were agreed before this ran; changing them afterwards makes the agreement meaningless.
*Floor:* **yes** · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* the author's ruling of 2026-09-02; FAR 10.002(e).

### Handoff and housekeeping

**S14** — Hands on both the requirement set it read and its own findings.
*Fit:* the design phase can reach every finding and its reasoning without re-running the search, and can overturn one. A run that hands findings over as settled rather than as evidence fails.
*Why:* pre-searching is only equivalent to searching inside design if design still sees the alternatives and the reasons.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* the author's ruling of 2026-09-02.

**S2** — Voids findings once a requirement's meaning has changed, and re-runs against the new meaning.
*Fit:* every finding records which version of which requirement it was scored against. This is between runs, never within one: a run reads the set once, at the start.
*Why:* a requirement can narrow while keeping its number, and findings scored against the old meaning then look current. Both R22 and R4 did exactly that upstream.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 2 · *Source:* 29148 §5.2.8's Version Number attribute; the author's ruling of 2026-09-02.

**S25** — Runs on Claude Code and Codex.
*Fit:* the body invokes only mechanisms both harnesses provide, and a fresh install on each produces one complete findings artifact from one run.
*Why:* a skill that works on one harness fails silently on the other — #56 recorded 8,873 bytes of guidance arriving absent with nothing raised.
*Floor:* no · *Version:* 1 · *Source:* promoted from a constraint on 2026-09-03; fails 29148 §3.1.7, since supporting both could have been decided otherwise.

## Constraints

29148 §3.1.7: a constraint is an *"externally imposed limitation… imposed on the solution by force or compulsion"*. Anything that could have been decided otherwise is a requirement.

- **A candidate's own terms bound what can be done with it.** A component with no licence cannot be copied whatever it covers; only its idea can be taken, with credit. This is the constraint most specific to this skill, because its subject is other people's work.
- **MIT and Apache-2.0 attribution is owed wherever text is taken substantially.**
- **Archived on handoff, never maintained** — inherited from the workflow. A finding is true of a version, not of a project.

## Open questions

- **What shape is the findings artifact?** The requirements say what it must contain, not how it is laid out.
- **What is "actively maintained" worth?** The author's rule — *"the actively maintained is a quality mark. I don't use verbatim a section from a skill with 1 user"* — has published thresholds for its first half (OpenSSF Scorecard's *"at least one commit per week during the previous 90 days"*) and nothing for its second. No number was elicited and none is recorded.
- **Is the prior art deep enough?** Two standards were read before the requirements and five corpora after, which is the wrong order. Unsearched: COTS-selection literature beyond the methods surveyed, and any second source for S9.
