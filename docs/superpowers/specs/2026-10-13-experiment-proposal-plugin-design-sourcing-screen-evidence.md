# Agent skills similar to the experiment proposal guide — sourcing-screen evidence

- **Date:** surveyed 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-13
- **Method:** as the survey records under Method below (unchanged)
- **Spec:** [2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design.md](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design.md)

*Compares existing agent skills with How to Write an Experiment Project Proposal (the guide; now docs/guides/experiment-proposal-guide.md, planned).*

## Summary

No existing agent skill implements the guide's 13-step process end to end; each of the 167 verified skills covers a slice. The recommendation is to borrow rather than adopt a skill chain.

Of the twelve closest matches, four stand out; to see one's quotes, gaps, and Reuse line, follow its name:

- The [HCI researcher](#human-computer-interaction-researcher-k-dense-aiscientific-agents) (K-Dense-AI's human-computer-interaction-researcher) is the only HCI-calibrated skill. It adds judgment, not drafting, across steps 1, 2, 6 to 9, and 11.
- [proposal-write](#proposal-write-hutzelmannthesis-proposal-skills) (hutzelmann) is the only verified skill that drafts a whole proposal under a controlled-experiment contract (steps 3 to 7, with parts of 1, 8, and 11). Its defaults leave out work plans and an appendix by design.
- [research-grill](#research-grill-scdenneyopen-science-skills) (scdenney) is the only verified defend stage aimed at a proposal (step 13).
- [cognitive-paradigm-design](#cognitive-paradigm-design-neuroaihubawesome_cognitive_and_neuroscience_skills) (NeuroAIHub) treats step 7's counterbalancing at lab-experiment grade, but it is AI-generated and written for cognitive psychology rather than HCI.

The thinnest steps are 2 (a session estimate that counts conditions), 3 (the experiment-proposal layout), and 10 (a verbatim script for a controlled experiment); for the nearest cover of each, see [Notes on the map](#notes-on-the-map).

What to do next: fold the [ideas for the guide](#ideas-for-the-guide) into the guide or into a future skill built from it. When building that skill, read proposal-write and its siblings, and the obra/superpowers brainstorming and writing-plans skills under [Process models from software development](#process-models-from-software-development-obrasuperpowers), as structural models. Use the HCI researcher for HCI-specific design judgment. Before quoting, copying, or installing from any source, read [Before reusing](#before-reusing) for the license terms, the K-Dense citation request, and shared runtime limits.

## Conventions

- **Steps.** Numbers refer to the guide's steps: 1 Fit and ethics · 2 Plan · 3 Document setup · 4 Introduction · 5 Literature review · 6 Research questions or hypotheses · 7 Design · 8 Participants · 9 Materials · 10 Procedure · 11 Planned analysis · 12 Appendix · 13 Review and defend. "Done-when" means the guide's per-step completion lists.
- **Verified.** A skill is verified when its file was read in full or at section level and it has an entry here or in the rejected list; 167 skills are verified. A claim that something is missing means missing among the verified skills.
- **Closest match, partial match, rejected.** Each verified skill is rated only on how much of the experiment-planning and proposal-writing process it implements: a closest match rates high, a partial match medium, and a rejected skill low or not relevant. To check why a skill was rejected, see the [rejected list](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-rejected-skills-evidence.md#rejected-skills). Licenses, runtime requirements, invocation mode, and portability are reported as facts and do not affect ratings. Differences from the guide's format or conventions are treated as design differences, not defects.
- **Covers, in part.** In an entry's Covers line and in the coverage map, a skill covers a step (covers it well) when it treats the step's main content, and covers it in part when it touches the step.
- **Citations.** Every factual claim about a skill links to that skill's own `SKILL.md`, or to a reference file in the same skill folder when the claim comes from there. Quotations are exact. "Last updated" means the last commit touching that file.
- **Inference.** Ratings, coverage judgments, and recommendations are mine. They are inferences, marked "(inference)" where they could be mistaken for a source's claim and "(inference, verify)" where they were not checked against an outside source.
- **AI-generated.** The skill's frontmatter sets `review_status: "ai-generated"`: check its parameters and citations before relying on them.
- **Defend stage.** An interactive step-13 rehearsal in which the agent puts a reviewer's objections to the researcher, who settles each one.
- **Controlled-experiment contract.** The elements a skill requires a controlled experiment's method to state, such as hypotheses, variables, design, assignment, and planned tests.
- **Names.** Each skill keeps one display name throughout. A bare name gets its owner as a prefix when another skill in this report shares it, for example K-Dense-AI statistical-analysis or oakoss usability-tester. Diverga and scriptorium skills carry their collection's name, and K-Dense-AI human-computer-interaction-researcher is called the HCI researcher. Owners are capitalized as GitHub reports them.

## What to borrow

An idea can be worth borrowing from a skill that does not match the guide overall. Before copying a source's wording, check its license under [Before reusing](#before-reusing). When revising step 13, also see [What carries over](#what-carries-over) for checks drawn from obra/superpowers.

### Ideas for the guide

An idea counts as borrowed when the guide step it names states it, as guidance or as a Done-when item.

01. **Tiered recording consent (steps 1 and 12).** [informed-consent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattArtzAnthro/AI-Anthropology-Toolkit/HEAD/skills/informed-consent/SKILL.md) requires that participants can take part without being recorded. This extends step 1's data minimization to screen and video recording in HCI sessions. Diverga [d2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brycewang-stanford/Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills/a6daaf2f27f49ec7814db69bafd5239a022ad5ae/skills/25-HosungYou-Diverga/skills/d2/SKILL.md) adds a consent item for quotations, "Permission to use in publications (verbatim, paraphrased, anonymized)", which matters when a study quotes post-session interviews.
02. **Withdrawal and retention with a deadline and an owner (steps 1 and 12).** [the-ethics-board](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mohitagw15856/pm-claude-skills/main/skills/the-ethics-board/SKILL.md) holds that "Withdrawal has mechanics, not sentiment." It scores "Of course they can withdraw" as a promise, while "a procedure with a deadline is scored as a protocol", and its checklist requires that "Withdrawal and retention each get deadlines and named owners or a revision entry". Step 1's data-minimization item and the step 12 consent text could name the date until which a participant can withdraw their data, the destruction date, and who carries out each. Diverga [d2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brycewang-stanford/Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills/a6daaf2f27f49ec7814db69bafd5239a022ad5ae/skills/25-HosungYou-Diverga/skills/d2/SKILL.md) gives such a deadline ("withdraw from the study up to 2 weeks after the interview"), and [euxlab-setup](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roxanneallard/euxlab-skills/HEAD/skills/loop/euxlab-setup/SKILL.md) is not complete until "the consent posture names a retention period and an identification scheme".
03. **Pilot data out of the inferential sample (step 2).** The [HCI researcher](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agents/HEAD/scientific-agents/human-computer-interaction-researcher/skills/human-computer-interaction-researcher/SKILL.md) keeps pilots out of the inferential n. The guide allows reporting pilot data only when the pilot runs exactly as proposed; stating the default (pilot data stay out of the main sample) would remove an ambiguity.
04. **A scope verdict for the whole study (step 2).** [proposal-supervise](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills/main/skills/proposal-supervise/SKILL.md) tables each work package with "a coarse estimate in weeks marked as an estimate", sets the total against the months in the plan, and closes on one line: "Scope verdict: fits", tight, or over-scoped. Step 2 checks that the session fits the time available; the same one-line verdict could check that recruiting, the pilot, the sessions, and the analysis fit the term.
05. **An argument sketch before the prose (steps 4 and 5).** [drafting-manuscript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leiverkus/research-superpowers/HEAD/skills/drafting-manuscript/SKILL.md) settles each section's argument before drafting: the sketch names the evidence, a counter-position ("A section that cannot name what argues against it has not engaged the debate"), and a "Not here" line against drift, because "Redirecting twenty lines is free; redirecting a thousand words is not". The Introduction and Literature Review could start from such a sketch, agreed with the supervisor before the prose.
06. **A named claim for every source (step 5).** [proposal-lit-search](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills/main/skills/proposal-lit-search/SKILL.md) lets an entry into the references "only when you can name the claim it will support there", logs rejected candidates, and stops broad searching once every research question has an accepted source or a round adds nothing. Step 5 already asks for snowballing until it stops turning up new work; its Done-when could add that each cited source supports a named claim in the review.
07. **Equivalence tests for "no worse than" predictions (step 6).** [hypothesis-building](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/main/plugin/skills/hypothesis-building/SKILL.md) treats TOST equivalence tests and a smallest effect size of interest as standard options. Step 6 says each hypothesis "can be tested against a null hypothesis of no difference". An HCI study that expects a new technique to be no slower cannot support that claim with a non-significant result. One bullet could point to equivalence testing.
08. **Record the random seed and the generated schedule (step 7).** [K-Dense-AI experimental-design](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/main/skills/experimental-design/SKILL.md) records the design and seed and generates allocation schedules with a script. Step 7 asks for "a truly random source"; recording the seed, or putting the generated order list in the Appendix, makes the assignment reproducible. Its [design_types reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/main/skills/experimental-design/references/design_types.md) recommends a Williams square to balance order. The balanced square in the guide's background section is what the reference calls a Williams square (inference), and the name is worth adding there because readers can search for it.
09. **Flag operational definitions without a source (step 7).** [define-variables](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Aperivue/medsci-skills/HEAD/skills/define-variables/SKILL.md) maps each variable to a cited definition and marks every row in an `Ad-hoc?` column, with a justification for each yes. Step 7 could ask that each operational definition cite a prior study or be marked as the writer's own choice with a reason.
10. **Contingency plans set in advance (steps 8 and 11).** [pre-registration-writing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/HEAD/plugin/skills/pre-registration-writing/SKILL.md) binds responses to attrition, sample shortfall, and failed manipulation checks before data arrive. The guide covers no-shows with extras (step 8) but does not say what happens if recruitment falls short.
11. **Effect-size sourcing and a sensitivity range (step 8).** [statistical-power](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/main/skills/statistical-power/SKILL.md) ranks effect-size sources, reports a range instead of one number, and inflates enrolled n for dropout. Step 8's Done-when could require the effect size's source and the n across a range of plausible effects. K-Dense-AI hypothesis-generation's [design reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/main/skills/hypothesis-generation/references/experimental_design_patterns.md) adds that "Pilot data may be too unstable for definitive effect-size planning", which qualifies step 8's plan to let the pilot refine the number.
12. **Named reasons for omitting a power analysis (step 8).** [methods-reporting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/main/plugin/skills/methods-reporting/SKILL.md) uses Lakens' six types of sample-size justification. Step 8 accepts "a reason for omitting one"; naming the accepted kinds of reason (for example resource constraints, or a heuristic from similar studies) would make that item checkable.
13. **Third-party recruitment of your own students (step 8).** [alterlab-research-ethics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlterLab-IEU/AlterLab-Academic-Skills/HEAD/skills/research-tools/alterlab-research-ethics/SKILL.md) pairs that risk with a remedy: recruitment by a third party and opt-out without penalty. Step 8 names the risk but not a remedy.
14. **Which effect size feeds the power analysis (step 8).** [cogsci-power-analysis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NeuroAIHub/awesome_cognitive_and_neuroscience_skills/master/skills/cogsci-power-analysis/SKILL.md) (AI-generated) warns that "Between-subjects Cohen's d and within-subjects d_z are not interchangeable." For a within-subjects design, step 8 could require the power analysis to say which of the two it uses and where the value came from, since an effect size taken from a between-subjects study does not transfer unchanged.
15. **Ordinal data and questionnaire caveats (steps 9 and 11).** The [HCI researcher](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agents/HEAD/scientific-agents/human-computer-interaction-researcher/skills/human-computer-interaction-researcher/SKILL.md) recommends cumulative link models for Likert data. It warns that SUS should be benchmarked against domain norms and "does not localize problems", and that NASA-TLX workload is contested. Step 9 could note these limits next to its recommendation of standard questionnaires. Step 11's test table could mention ordinal regression as a modern alternative for single-item Likert outcomes.
16. **Decision rules per hypothesis (step 11, sharpening step 6's list of possible outcomes).** [pre-registration-writing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/HEAD/plugin/skills/pre-registration-writing/SKILL.md) requires, for each confirmatory hypothesis, what counts as "support, falsification, or an inconclusive result." A Done-when item in step 11 could require each hypothesis's test to state which result supports it, which contradicts it, and which is inconclusive.
17. **Run the analysis script on simulated or pilot data before collection (step 11).** [pre-registration-writing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/HEAD/plugin/skills/pre-registration-writing/SKILL.md) registers "analysis code run on simulated data". This is a mechanism rather than a rule: a script that runs end to end on fake data proves the planned analysis is complete. It fits the guide's pilot (step 2).
18. **Fallback chosen in advance by the assumption check (step 11).** [alterlab-test-selection-guard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlterLab-IEU/AlterLab-Academic-Skills/HEAD/skills/methodology/alterlab-test-selection-guard/SKILL.md) states the rule in one line. Step 11 already asks for "the rule for choosing between them"; that one line is a ready example of such a rule.
19. **Researcher degrees of freedom listed with their rules (step 11).** [research-literacy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NeuroAIHub/awesome_cognitive_and_neuroscience_skills/master/skills/research-literacy/references/planning-template.md) (AI-generated) has an analysis-pipeline table in which each step is marked as a researcher degree of freedom or not, with a justification. Step 11 says all of it is pre-specified; such a table would show where a choice (an exclusion, a transformation, a test) could be made after seeing the data, and the rule that fixes it in advance.
20. **A three-way disposition for feedback (step 13).** [research-grill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scdenney/open-science-skills/HEAD/plugin/skills/research-grill/SKILL.md) resolves each objection as answered, accepted as a limitation, or turned into a design change. Step 13 asks that each piece of feedback be "recorded with what you did about it"; those three outcomes make the record uniform.
21. **Aims, methods, budget, and timeline that agree (steps 2 and 13).** [pedrohcgs grant-proposal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/main/.claude/skills/grant-proposal/SKILL.md) runs a coherence pass on the principle that "Aims, methods, budget, timeline, and broader impacts must agree with each other and with the underlying research spec." For example, "an RCT aim implies a participant-incentives line". The step 13 cross-section check could include the plan from step 2: every condition, measure, and compensation the Method names appears in the session estimate and any budget, and everything there appears in the Method.

### Mechanisms for a future skill

For a future skill built from the guide, rather than the guide itself, five mechanisms recur:

- **Placeholders instead of invented content.** [preregister](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/HEAD/.claude/skills/preregister/SKILL.md) writes `[CLARIFY: <specific question>]` for any missing required field, and [write-protocol](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Aperivue/medsci-skills/HEAD/skills/write-protocol/SKILL.md) leaves institution-specific sections as `[TODO]` and says to "never fill them with invented institutional content." [proposal-write](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills/main/skills/proposal-write/SKILL.md) adds a checker: its bundled `check.py` reports open `[TODO: …]` markers along with structural errors, the agent must run it before it reports a pass, and the agent may not delete a marker to clear the check. clo-author [strategize](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hugosantanna/clo-author/HEAD/.claude/skills/strategize/SKILL.md), which drafts pre-analysis plans (PAPs), marks every detail it had to assume as `[ASSUMED]` and lists them in a closing checklist, because "A registered PAP with unchecked assumptions is worse than no PAP."
- **Numbers come from executed code.** [calc-sample-size](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Aperivue/medsci-skills/HEAD/skills/calc-sample-size/SKILL.md): "the reported N is the number it prints, not a hand calculation." write-protocol also cross-checks the sample-size section against the analysis section.
- **Accountability lists and approval gates.** [irb-protocol](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MattArtzAnthro/AI-Anthropology-Toolkit/HEAD/skills/irb-protocol/SKILL.md) attaches "Unresolved" and "Assembled rather than authored" lists to each draft and refuses to classify risk for the researcher, which matches the guide's point that the institution decides exemption. [luwill research-proposal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luwill/research-skills/HEAD/research-proposal/SKILL.md) blocks drafting until the outline is approved.
- **A freeze enforced by a hook rather than a rule.** [prereg](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/elliottower/reproducible-science/HEAD/packages/prereg/plugin/skills/prereg/SKILL.md) hashes the plan and sets it read-only. "The plugin refuses an `Edit`, `Write` or `MultiEdit` to a frozen file", so later changes go to an append-only log or numbered amendments. Its rule is "**Freeze before you look. A frozen file never changes by one byte.**" A skill built from the guide could freeze the Planned Analysis the same way once the proposal is approved. The skill states the limit itself: "It also cannot tell you the plan was any good — a frozen bad design is still a bad design, registered."
- **A fresh reader for review output.** [proposal-supervise](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills/main/skills/proposal-supervise/SKILL.md) checks every claim in its feedback against its source and then starts "one fresh verifier of the finished feedback, worth its cost because it did not write what it checks." obra/superpowers brainstorming dispatches a fresh subagent to read a design as its builder (for how it sorts the builder's questions, see [brainstorming](#brainstorming)). A skill built from the guide could run its step 13 review and rehearsal the same way.

## Process models from software development (obra/superpowers)

[obra/superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers) is a software-development skill library, so none of its skills matches the guide. Two of them are still worth reading for structure and style: they run the same kind of process — work out what is wanted, write it down for someone who was not in the room, review it, then hand off — and they are widely used (for their star counts, see the [repository snapshot](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-repositories-evidence.md)). This section cites the current `main` versions, pinned to their latest commits.

### brainstorming

[Source: SKILL.md on main](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obra/superpowers/bb92a77741419a4ab5f06e711a283343f1ada0c3/skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md). Its description reaches beyond code, to "planning anything new, in software or out of it (a talk, a business, a renovation)". The agent first asks itself "what do I know about what they want, and why?", then asks one question per message and plays its understanding back in chunks of "about 200-300 words", marking guesses as guesses. It sizes the work (a quick task, a small change, or a project with a written design) and writes the design for "a talented builder in the domain" to plan from.

A fresh subagent then reads the design as that builder, and the agent sorts the builder's questions into three piles: answered already, minor (settle it and mark it "as your call"), and theirs (only the human can answer). A hard gate holds all building until the human approves.

### writing-plans

[Source: SKILL.md on main](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obra/superpowers/8ca22dba9a94f28898bbce59f2537ff4d87c747d/skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md). It turns an approved design into tasks for "an engineer who has not seen this codebase or this spec". The plan records "what you decided" and leaves the rest to the implementer: "A plan is the set of decisions the implementer cannot make alone." Every plan opens with a fixed header: goal, architecture, tech stack, a link to the spec, Global Constraints "copied verbatim from the spec", and a Review Focus list of the five failure modes the spec implies but no task's tests exercise.

Each task declares the interfaces it consumes and produces, and each step is "one action with a checkable result". The self-review checks spec coverage, scans for lines that decide nothing or transcribe the code, checks that names match across tasks, checks Review Focus, and checks proportion: "A plan several times longer than the spec it implements is a transcript of the program, not a plan."

### Structure and style compared

- **Split by sequence.** Superpowers runs the process as separate skills with explicit handoffs: brainstorming hands off only to writing-plans, which hands off to an execution skill. On main each is about 1,700 words. The guide puts planning, writing, and review in one document of about 4,900 words. A skill built from the guide could split the same way: design the experiment, write the proposal, and review it.
- **Fixed templates.** writing-plans shows its plan header and task skeleton in code blocks. The guide has no copyable proposal skeleton; one with section headings and a one-line prompt under each would give a drafter a starting file.
- **Prose.** Superpowers main writes short paragraphs with bold lead-ins, uses tables for decisions, and says "Use plain language" and "never use process jargon". The guide uses nested bullets throughout.

### What does not carry over

- **Software mechanics:** test-driven steps, commits, file paths, worktrees, type signatures, subagent-driven execution, and the visual companion.
- **Conversation mechanics:** one question per message, playback, and the offer to skip the questions fit an interactive skill that helps someone shape an idea, not a reference document.
- **Sizing paths:** a course pilot, a thesis study, and a preregistered study differ, but splitting the guide into paths would add branches for little gain.

## Closest matches

Twelve skills. The four the Summary names come first, in its order; the rest follow by main guide step, then by name.

### human-computer-interaction-researcher (K-Dense-AI/scientific-agents)

[Source: SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agents/HEAD/scientific-agents/human-computer-interaction-researcher/skills/human-computer-interaction-researcher/SKILL.md). An expert-persona skill that tells the agent to "Think and work like an expert Human–Computer Interaction Researcher". It sets judgment rather than generating a document. It frames a study in phases (frame, formative, build, pilot, collect, analyze, report). The pilot phase is "3–5 pilot sessions", and "Pilots are not included in inferential n." For design it prescribes "counterbalanced within-subjects designs with Latin squares; yoked or matched between-subjects when carryover is fatal."

For analysis it matches the statistics to the data type: "Likert and ordinal scales → cumulative link (mixed) models (CLMM), not raw t-tests on 1–7 means". It discusses SUS and NASA-TLX with validity caveats. ### Notes on partial matches

- **writing-irb-submissions consent elements (inference, verify).** The [skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CaseMark/skills/HEAD/skills/med/writing-irb-submissions/SKILL.md) lists "Eight Required Elements (45 CFR 46.116(b))". The 2018 revised Common Rule has nine basic elements and adds a key-information section, which the skill omits.
- **scdenney literature-review.** Its plugin variant sets `disable-model-invocation: true`, so it runs only when called by name.
- **proposal-lit-search (inference).** It is the strongest step-5 search mechanism verified.
- **scriptorium review, synthesize, and contradictions.** Every factual sentence of their literature-review prose resolves to a verbatim quote at a locator. Hedging is calibrated to study-design tier, disagreements are classed by construct, population, timeframe, and operationalization, and a shipped cite-check script gates the draft. The scope phase lists a grant proposal as a target. They run in Claude Cowork, and their search phase needs connected scholarly-search MCPs.
  - Design tiers (inference): [extract](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jerrymwolf/scriptorium-cowork/HEAD/skills/extract/SKILL.md) defines the `experimental` tier "with or without random assignment (RCT, quasi-experimental, single-case design all fold here)", yet [synthesize](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jerrymwolf/scriptorium-cowork/HEAD/skills/synthesize/SKILL.md) renders that tier as "In a randomized trial of [N] [population], X was associated with improved Y". Followed literally, the template calls a quasi-experiment or a single-case study a randomized trial. synthesize states its own limit: "The script does NOT enforce per-tier rules".
- **experiment-agent.** Its plan mode covers variables with role and scale, confounds, an instrument table, a phased timeline, an analysis table with a named fallback test, and an ethics block with consent method and retention. The upstream Claude repository, Imbad0202/academic-research-skills, has no `experiment-agent` folder; the agent survives only in the Codex build's vendored [WORKFLOW.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills-codex/HEAD/skills/academic-research-suite/ars/experiment-agent/WORKFLOW.md) (v1.1.0, 2026-05-02), and it runs inside [academic-research-suite](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills-codex/HEAD/skills/academic-research-suite/SKILL.md). Its plan mode is non-prescriptive: "plan mode helps the user clarify their thinking — it does not prescribe a specific design. The user makes all design decisions."

Welch's correction addresses unequal variances, not non-normality. The [SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NeuroAIHub/awesome_cognitive_and_neuroscience_skills/master/skills/research-literacy/SKILL.md) also attributes a "43%" rate of self-reported HARKing to Fiedler & Schwarz (2016), which was not checked against the paper.

## Method

- **Scope.** The target was existing skills in any agent format: Agent Skills `SKILL.md` files (Claude Code, Claude.ai, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, OpenClaw), Claude Code plugins that ship skills, and Codex skills. Topics were experiment and study design, research, grant, and thesis proposals, HCI and UX research planning, usability-test planning, IRB, consent, and ethics protocols, preregistration, power analysis, and statistical test selection.
- **Sources searched.** WebSearch queries (standard mode, with extended mode where results were thin) covered each topic, plus "SKILL.md" queries for HCI user studies, counterbalancing, NASA-TLX, SUS, OSF preregistration, thesis proposals, and Codex, Cursor, and Gemini CLI research skills. The registries skills.sh, claudskills.com, agentskill.sh, skillsmp, aitmpl, openskillindex, tessl, claudemarketplaces, playbooks.com, and similar sites all mirror the same GitHub repositories. The awesome lists read were VoltAgent/awesome-agent-skills, brycewang-stanford/Awesome-Agent-Skills-for-Empirical-Research (now Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills), travisvn/awesome-claude-skills, ComposioHQ/awesome-claude-skills, and neverbiasu/awesome-research-skills. Skill folders on the searching machine (326 `SKILL.md` files) and a cache of 3,252 plugin-directory listings were also searched by name and text.
- **Fetching.** Repository trees were listed through GitHub's unauthenticated REST tree endpoint and, once its rate limit ran out, through blobless shallow clones; about 45 repositories were listed in full. Skill and reference files were fetched raw from `raw.githubusercontent.com`. "Last updated" dates come from GitHub's per-path commits Atom feed or REST API. GitHub code search needs authentication and was not used. For stars and forks of every repository named here, see the [repository snapshot](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-repositories-evidence.md).
- **Quotations.** Quotations from 140 of the 167 skills were checked by search against the fetched file. For the 27 skills read first, spot checks on 2026-10-12 re-read the HCI researcher, memi-design usability-testing, luwill research-proposal, wentorai psychology-research-guide, K-Dense-AI experimental-design and statistical-analysis, and the scdenney frontmatter.
- **Not verified.** Skills seen only at description or heading level include most of the Auto-Empirical-Research-Skills mirror's 1,172 `SKILL.md` files: its copies of repositories covered here are stale vendored snapshots, so this report cites upstream, and a full-text search for human-participant terms found nothing in the rest outside Diverga. They also include the remaining skills of leiverkus, scriptorium, Erdemhan, euxlab, and ux-superpowers, which descriptions, headings, and term searches place outside the process. To check whether a skill was considered and why it was rejected, see [Rejected and unread skills](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-rejected-skills-evidence.md).
- **Limits.** Absence claims rest on searching each fetched file for named terms (for example counterbalancing, Latin square, within-subjects, NASA-TLX, SUS, consent, and power). Code snippets were read, not run. No Cursor `.mdc` rule for experiment design or research proposals was found, and the only Gemini CLI research extension found was poemswe/co-researcher's manifest. For renamed and empty repositories, see [Dead ends](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-rejected-skills-evidence.md#dead-ends); for files that bear on a verdict but were not read, see [Not read](2026-10-13-experiment-proposal-plugin-design-rejected-skills-evidence.md#not-read).
