# Background: qualitative methods

## What the Summary reports

How participants or sites were purposefully selected; what data was collected; the coding process; what it produced.

## What sound qualitative work looks like

1. Codes are interpretive.
2. Early codes stay close to the data — participants' language (in vivo) before abstracting.
3. Every analytic move is documented.
4. Interpretation is the authors' work, not an NLP tool's.

## Sampling and case selection

- **Saturation** is the goal. Typical: 6-12 interviews for narrow scope, 20-30 for grounded theory.
- **Purposive** is the default.
- A case study selects typical, diverse, or extreme cases; the question is how far the authors claim the resulting themes or model extend beyond the cases they chose.
- Participatory research gives participants a crucial role: helping set research priorities, collaborating on research artifacts, and learning about the results.

## Trustworthiness and reflexivity

Trustworthiness (Lincoln & Guba):

- **Credibility** — triangulation, member checking, prolonged engagement.
- **Transferability** — thick description.
- **Dependability** — audit trail of decisions.
- **Confirmability** — reflexivity statement on the authors' positionality: who the authors are, their relationship to the topic and participants, what biases they bring.

## Analytic traditions, and what each should report

| Tradition | What it asks | Data | What the paper should report |
|-----------|--------------|------|------------------------------|
| **Reflexive thematic analysis** (Braun & Clarke) | What themes recur in the data? | Any qualitative data | Familiarization; initial coding across the corpus; theme construction; theme review against coded data and the full corpus; theme defining and naming, a one-sentence essence each; an interpretive narrative with extracts as evidence |
| **Grounded theory** (Charmaz / Strauss-Corbin) | What theory explains this process? | Interviews + observation | Open coding line-by-line; axial coding into categories; selective coding to the core category; theoretical sampling; constant comparison; continuation to theoretical saturation |
| **Phenomenology / IPA** | What is the lived experience of X? | In-depth interviews | Idiographic order, one transcript fully before the next; descriptive → linguistic → conceptual comments; superordinate themes within case, then patterns across cases |
| **Framework analysis** (Ritchie & Spencer) | What do pre-defined topics look like across cases? | Interviews, structured qualitative data | A thematic framework from familiarization; every transcript indexed against it; data charted into a matrix (rows = participants, columns = themes) |
| **Content analysis** (Krippendorff / Hsieh & Shannon) | How often and in what context does X appear? | Texts, transcripts, media | Which variant — conventional (codes emerge), directed (theory-derived codes, refined), or summative (count, then interpret context) — plus the reliability it claims |
| **Ethnography** | What is going on in this culture/setting? | Participant observation, field notes | Thick description, cultural pattern analysis, engagement duration and role in the setting |
| **Narrative inquiry** | What stories do people tell? | Life histories, narrative interviews | Structural and thematic narrative analysis |
| **Discourse analysis** | How is X talked about or represented? | Texts, transcripts, media | Coding of language patterns, and the corpus boundary |

## Inter-rater reliability and the self-audit a paper should pass

With two or more coders: both code a subset (typically 20-30% of transcripts); Cohen's κ for two coders, Fleiss' κ for 3+, or Krippendorff's α. Targets: κ ≥ 0.80 strong, 0.60-0.79 substantial, 0.40-0.59 moderate.

Does the paper show:

- Codebook consistency — every code has a clear definition and discriminating examples.
- Themes with a clear central organizing concept (not just topic buckets).
- Each theme supported by quotes from multiple participants (or the limit acknowledged).
- Disconfirming cases addressed.
- Reflexivity statement included.
- An audit trail reconstructable from memos and codebook versions.
- If NLP or LLM-assisted coding was used, validation against hand-coding documented.
