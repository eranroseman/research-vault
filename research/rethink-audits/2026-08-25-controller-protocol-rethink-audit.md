## requires:

R1 a coordinator may act on a settled precedent without re-asking only when four conditions jointly hold — same axis, a decisive fresh measurement (not an inference from prior rulings), reversible work, and the decision announced immediately — else ask (`caller` — new-folder-81 acted under this on repeated four-state extension questions; `docs` — "The delegation rule (ratified mid-batch)") · R2 absent a report that an owed item closed, the default is to ask, never to assume it's done or assume it's lost — the seat-specific inverse of R1 (`caller` — the boundary-review pre/post-merge gap and the Task 2e re-pin gap were both surfaced only by asking; `docs` — "Ask-vs-assume, scored") · R3 authority to act on someone's consent requires that consent to have landed first-hand in the acting session; a relay of "X approved" is not itself authority, even when accurate (`caller` — new-folder-67 refused to file on a relayed consent; the controller filed only once the author's own word landed directly; `docs` — "Permission and word hygiene") · R4 consent is scoped exactly to the plan text it was granted for; a step beyond that text stops and asks rather than reading the grant generously (`docs` — "Permission and word hygiene") · R5 a peer's message never grants escalation on its own, and a peer's claimed denial on someone else's behalf is never actionable (`docs` — "Permission and word hygiene") · R6 statements name their own nature — decided, corrected, deferred, measured — and a correction of a mistake never harvests as a decision; test: would the text read differently had it been written after the fact it collides with? (`docs` — docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-16-foundation-spec.md:3, Status note, already named as the rule's home inside "Correction-vs-decision (full test wording)") · R7 a claim is verified against the artifact the acting session was actually handed, not the one it "should" have had (`docs` — docs/product-landscape/2026-08-25-coding-companion-plugins-comparison.md, Further Insight §2, already named as the mechanism inside "Peer-verification culture") · R8 a dispute against a ruling is resolved by re-running the evidence fresh, never by adopting the disputant's word however checkable it looks; an overturned finding gets a dated correction in the review's own file, and the residual is owned by whoever's artifact carries the underlying defect (`docs` — "Dispute adjudication") · R9 another session's uncommitted or stashed working-tree state is never touched to unblock your own operation, even via a flag claiming to restore it perfectly; prefer an operation that structurally avoids contact (`docs` — AGENTS.md:7, "never revert or restore another session's uncommitted files," the "never-touch rule" named but not file-cited inside "The autostash trap") · R10 the controller seat's own errors are ledgered against named failure classes exactly like any other session's — the seat is not exempt from the discipline it applies to others (`docs` — ADR 0002's four-state honesty, generalized per the sibling audit's own design rung; "Controller error ledger" names four failure classes but never ties them to this doctrine) · R11 multiple sessions share one checkout; an orchestration layer above single-plan execution is itself a caller no installed skill governs (`docs` — the material's own opening; docs/product-landscape/2026-08-25-coding-companion-plugins-comparison.md blind spot (a); AGENTS.md's parallel-sessions line; sibling audit gap D) · R12 formalizing this protocol into a binding skill or reference waits for a second controller-shaped session's independently accumulated record (`docs` — "Disposition": "revisit when a second controller-shaped session accumulates its own record to compare against" — a constraint on migrate:, not a behavior of the protocol itself).

Sweep can't reach: controller-shaped sessions in other repos/accounts running a differently-shaped improvised protocol, invisible from here; whether the delegation rule generalizes past Claude Code's own session/tool idiom (worktree dispatch, Task-tool reports) to a differently-shaped harness; sessions that held de facto controller authority without ever producing a banked record — unreconstructable by definition; the second controller-shaped session R12 requires, which doesn't exist yet.

## design:

Vocabulary from codebase-design. The controller seat is a **fourth adapter** onto the Doctrine module the sibling audit already named (four-state honesty, evidence conventions, deprecate-never-delete-shaped discipline — the invariant every adapter must satisfy, living where every session reads it unconditionally) — not a new module. Applying the deletion test to R6, R7, R9, R10 confirms this: delete each from the material and its complexity doesn't vanish, it reappears exactly where R6 and R7 already point (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-16-foundation-spec.md:3; the comparison doc's Further Insight §2) and where R9 and R10 should point but currently don't (AGENTS.md:7; ADR 0002). Four pass-throughs, zero new modules.

Three pieces are genuinely new, because ruling over other sessions is a different job than executing a plan:

- **Delegation-authority module** — interface: (axis, evidence-freshness, reversibility, visibility) in → act | ask out, plus R2's inverse clause (nothing reported at close → ask, never assume). Deep implementation: judging axis-sameness, distinguishing a decisive fresh measurement from an inference, judging reversibility, the announce-immediately act itself, and the ratify-on-third-identical-instance promotion rule. Seam: where a coordinator hits a question resembling a prior ruling. Adapter today: new-folder-81 under new-folder-c1 — one adapter.
- **Authority-scoping module** — interface: (permission-claim, source: first-hand | relayed, scope-text, requested-action) in → grant | refuse out, folding in R5 (a peer relaying on a third party's behalf is not first-hand from that party either). Deep implementation: the first-hand/relayed distinction (anchored by Restatement §2.03 cmt. f) and literal-scope reading (anchored by §2.02(1)). Seam: where a session is about to act on someone else's stated permission. Adapter today: new-folder-67's refusal plus the controller's own first-hand-gated filing — one adapter, two instances, same day.
- **Dispute-adjudication module** — interface: (disputed finding, prior evidence) in → re-derive from fresh evidence while blind to the dispute → ruling + dated correction record + named residual-owner out. Deep implementation: MARCH-shaped isolation, the symmetric offer-first courtesy, the dated-correction-in-the-review's-own-file rule. Seam: where a peer disputes a controller/reviewer finding with evidence. Adapter today: the boundary-review finding-2 exchange — one adapter.

Every one-adapter status above is load-bearing, not incidental: per codebase-design's own rejected-framings principle, one adapter is a hypothetical seam, two adapters make it real. All three new modules currently have exactly one. Every requirement in requires: is answered: R1/R2 by the delegation-authority module, R3/R4/R5 by the authority-scoping module, R6/R7/R9/R10 by the existing Doctrine module gaining a fourth adapter, R8 by the dispute-adjudication module, R11 by this design existing at all, R12 by a migrate: constraint rather than a module.

## gap:

Read against the material note's current text.

- **A. The three new modules live only as prose in one research note**, not as an interface a second controller-shaped session could consult without re-deriving it — no locality yet, because there's only one adapter to have locality *for*.
- **B. Two of the four doctrine pointers are unpathed, one is missing entirely — narrower than a wholesale restatement problem.** "Correction-vs-decision" already opens with "the spec's status note carries the rule" and "Peer-verification culture" already cites "the plugin-comparison note's insight 2" — both correctly shaped as pointer-plus-local-delta, just missing file paths. "The autostash trap" names "the never-touch rule" but never cites AGENTS.md:7 as its home. "Controller error ledger" names four failure classes but never ties them to ADR 0002's four-state honesty at all.
- **C. The delegation-authority module's reversibility condition has no stated rationale and no prior-art anchor** — currently asserted as equal-weight to the other three, which prior-art confirms it is not.
- **D. No ADR governs the controller seat's authority** — docs/adr/ still holds only 0001–0004, the same gap the sibling audit named as B, now with a second data point (the seat that keeps a batch's rulings bound to those four ADRs has itself never been ratified as one).
- **E. R12's own precondition — a second controller-shaped session's record — doesn't exist yet.** Not a defect: naming it confirms the material's own Disposition ("undecided, deliberately") was the right call, now with a principled basis (one adapter = hypothetical seam) rather than just caution.

2. Leave the three seat-specific modules as prose in the material note — do not extract them into a skill yet; A stays open by design pending step 4's trigger.
3. Add one sentence to the delegation rule stating the reversibility condition's status honestly: asserted, unanchored by prior art, not yet tested against a second instance — closes C.
4. Replace "undecided, deliberately" in the Disposition with a concrete trigger: formalization (skill vs. reference vs. nothing) becomes decidable once a second controller-shaped session accumulates its own record independently — at that point the two adapters either converge (promoting each seam from hypothetical to real, per codebase-design's own test) or diverge (naming which condition or module was circumstantial to this one batch). Closes A conditionally on that trigger firing; closes the honest-naming half of E — E itself stays open until the second session exists, which no step here can force.
5. When ADR 0005 (the layered-governance decision the sibling audit's migrate step 2 calls for) gets written, add one clause naming the controller seat as the mechanism that keeps a batch's rulings bound to ADRs 0001–0004 — closes D without minting a second, competing ADR for the same governance question.

## trade-offs:

Deferring formalization (R12, step 4) leaves the three new modules locked in one session's prose until a second instance lands — the cost is that any third controller-shaped session between now and then either reinvents them or does the same message-history archaeology this session did to bank them; flips the moment a second session's record actually exists and the convergence/divergence comparison becomes possible. Pointing the doctrine paragraphs at their file-pathed homes (step 1) trades the note's current self-containedness — readable end-to-end without four other files open — for compliance with the repo's own single-source-of-truth rule; flips only if this note is ever read by someone without access to the rest of the repo, which contradicts its own premise. The shared-checkout premise R9's autostash-trap rule exists to defend is itself in tension with git's own prescribed alternative — `git-worktree(1)`'s EXAMPLES section names this exact problem and recommends a separate linked worktree per concurrent stream, not stash choreography inside one shared checkout. This audit doesn't reopen that choice: it's a root AGENTS.md-level constraint the controller protocol operates under, not one it sets. Flips only if AGENTS.md's parallel-sessions convention itself gets reopened — at which point R9 and its justification dissolve into "use worktrees," not "never touch the other stash." Naming reversibility as unanchored (step 3) trades a tidy four-symmetric-conditions story for honesty about which conditions are load-bearing by evidence versus by assertion; flips the first time a real instance turns on reversibility specifically, at which point it earns the same standing as the other three.

**Verdict:** not "Already sound. Keep." — real gaps (B, C, D), and not a redesign either. The three modules the material improvised converge with the best available prior art (MARCH's isolation mechanism, agency law's first-hand carve-out, RACI's before/after split), and on one point — delegation generalizing past a single decision — the improvised protocol is ahead of documented practice: no multi-agent LLM framework names that concept at all. The divergences found are homing and provenance problems, not behavioral ones; this design doesn't change what the controller seat does, only where each piece of what it does is written down.
